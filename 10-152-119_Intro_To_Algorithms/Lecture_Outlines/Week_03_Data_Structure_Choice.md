# 4. Opening Frame

Today we are moving from "How fast does it grow?" to "What structure makes this problem easier to solve?"

---

# 5. Course Positioning

Students now connect correctness and performance to how information is stored, accessed, and changed.

---

# 6. Core Concepts

- List: ordered collection, useful for sequence and iteration.
- Dictionary: key-based lookup and updating.
- Set: membership and uniqueness.
- Stack and queue: controlled access order.
- Representation fit: choosing based on operations, not habit.

---

# 7. Algorithm Visibility / Demo Plan

Demo attendance tracking with a list and dictionary.

Show:

- adding a record
- looking up a record
- updating a count
- side-by-side operation comparison

Evidence:

- comparison table
- small representation diagram

---

# 8. Hands-On / Lab Bridge

Students begin Lab 3 by selecting a different scenario and comparing two structures.

Their goal is not to use the most advanced structure. Their goal is to explain why a structure fits the operations.

---

# 9. Common Mistakes / Watch-Fors

- saying dictionaries are "always better"
- ignoring ordering requirements
- comparing structures without naming operations
- using data examples that are too small to reveal differences

---

# 10. AI Use Frame

AI may suggest alternate structures only after students choose and explain their first two structures.

Students must accept or reject AI suggestions based on access pattern.

---

# 11. Explain / Checkpoint Questions

- What operation matters most in your scenario?
- Which structure supports that operation clearly?
- What does the structure make harder?
- How would the code change if the data grew?

---

# 12. End-of-Class Success Check

By the end of this session, students should be able to justify a data structure choice using operations and tradeoffs.
