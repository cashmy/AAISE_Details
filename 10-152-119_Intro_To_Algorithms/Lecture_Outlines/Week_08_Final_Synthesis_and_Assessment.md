# 6. Core Concepts

- Working solution: meaningful evidence, even if imperfect.
- Explanation defense: verification of understanding, not a gotcha.
- Tradeoff: what the solution gains and gives up.
- Limitation: where the solution may fail or mislead.
- AI accountability: what AI contributed and what the student verified.

# 8. Hands-On / Lab Bridge

Students complete short formative practice prompts.

Their goal is not to produce another full lab. Their goal is to rehearse the kind of explanation they will need for the final.

---

# 9. Common Mistakes / Watch-Fors

- explaining what the code prints but not why it works
- hiding AI use instead of accounting for it
- claiming a solution is best without criteria
- forgetting to discuss assumptions or limitations
- treating poor but working code as worthless instead of improvable

---

# 10. AI Use Frame

AI may help students rehearse explanations or critique assumptions when allowed.

Students must still be able to explain their own final submission without outsourcing ownership.

---

# 11. Explain / Checkpoint Questions

- What does your solution do from input to output?
- What evidence shows it works?
- What assumption does it depend on?
- What would make it fail or behave poorly?
- What did AI help with, if anything?
- What would you improve with more time?

---

# 12. End-of-Class Success Check

By the end of this session, students should be able to describe what the final requires and practice explaining a solution with evidence, assumptions, tradeoffs, and AI accountability.
