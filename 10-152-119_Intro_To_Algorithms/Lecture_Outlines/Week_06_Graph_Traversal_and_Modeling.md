---

# 6. Core Concepts

- Node: an item or location in the graph.
- Edge: a relationship or connection.
- Adjacency list: a practical representation of neighbors.
- BFS: explores outward by layers.
- DFS: follows a path deeply before backtracking.

---

# 7. Algorithm Visibility / Demo Plan

Demo a campus route map.

Show:

- small graph diagram
- adjacency list
- BFS traversal order
- DFS traversal order

Evidence:

- traversal table with queue or stack state
- graph diagram

---

# 8. Hands-On / Lab Bridge

Students begin Lab 6 with a different relationship or movement system.

Their goal is not to draw a perfect map. Their goal is to represent relationships clearly enough to explain traversal behavior.

---

# 9. Common Mistakes / Watch-Fors

- missing bidirectional edges when needed
- listing nodes but not relationships
- assuming BFS and DFS always produce the same order
- forgetting that traversal depends on neighbor order

---

# 10. AI Use Frame

AI may critique the graph or explain BFS/DFS vocabulary after students draft their own model.

AI should not replace student graph design.

---

# 11. Explain / Checkpoint Questions

- What are the nodes?
- What are the edges?
- What does the graph leave out?
- How does BFS differ from DFS here?
- What does traversal order reveal?

---

# 12. End-of-Class Success Check

By the end of this session, students should be able to represent a small system as a graph and explain traversal evidence.
