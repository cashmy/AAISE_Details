# 6. Core Concepts

- Representation: how items become comparable data.
- Similarity: a rule for judging how alike two items are.
- Ranking: ordering items by a score.
- Clustering: grouping items based on closeness or similarity.
- Hashing: producing a stable value for lookup, identity, or integrity.

---

# 8. Hands-On / Lab Bridge

Students begin Lab 7 by selecting or receiving one approved option.

Their goal is not to create a sophisticated AI system. Their goal is to make one algorithmic idea visible and explain what the result can and cannot claim.

---

# 9. Common Mistakes / Watch-Fors

- treating a similarity score as truth
- using features without explaining why they matter
- forgetting that missing data changes results
- accepting AI-generated ranking logic without validation

---

# 10. AI Use Frame

AI may critique assumptions or suggest an alternate representation after students define their data and first algorithm idea.

Students must validate AI suggestions against their evidence.

---

# 11. Explain / Checkpoint Questions

- What representation did you choose?
- What assumption shapes the result?
- What would change if one feature were weighted differently?
- What can this result claim?
- What can this result not claim?

---

# 12. End-of-Class Success Check

By the end of this session, students should be able to explain how a small data/AI algorithm result depends on representation and assumptions.
