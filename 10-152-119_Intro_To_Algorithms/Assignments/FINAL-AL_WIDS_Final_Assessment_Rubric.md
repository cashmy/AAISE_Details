# WIDS FINAL ASSESSMENT RUBRIC

**10-152-119 Algorithmic Problem Solving**

---

# Purpose

This rubric supports the two-part final assessment for `10-152-119 Algorithmic
Problem Solving`.

It is designed to be WIDS-friendly and easy for non-technical reviewers to
follow. The rubric is divided into the same two parts as the final assessment:

1. **Part 1 - Applied Solution Set**
2. **Part 2 - Personalized Explanation Defense**

This rubric intentionally uses only technical course competencies. It does not
include CORE abilities for this final assessment.

# Performance Scale

| Score | Level | General Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 4 | Exceeds | Work is accurate, complete, well-supported, and clearly explained |
| 3 | Meets | Work satisfies the requirement with minor gaps that do not prevent assessment |

# Part 1 Rubric - Applied Solution Set

**Weight:** 70% of final assessment

Part 1 evaluates the submitted algorithmic work. Working solutions carry the
largest share of this score because producing functioning algorithmic artifacts
is the primary evidence for this course.

| Criterion | WIDS Alignment | Weight | 4 - Exceeds | 3 - Meets | 2 - Developing | 1 - Needs Improvement | 0 - Missing |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Functional algorithmic solutions | CC3 | 30% | Solutions run or simulate correctly, address all assigned tasks, and are readable enough to review | Solutions address the assigned tasks with minor errors or incomplete cases | Solutions are partially functional but contain logic gaps, incomplete tasks, or fragile behavior | Solutions are mostly nonfunctional or not meaningfully connected to the tasks | No meaningful solution submitted |
| Problem framing and assumptions | CC1 | 10% | Clearly identifies inputs, outputs, constraints, assumptions, and relevant edge cases | Identifies the main problem elements with minor gaps | Provides partial framing, but important assumptions or edge cases are missing | Provides little clear problem framing before implementation | No framing submitted |
| Data structure and approach fit | CC2 | 10% | Selects structures and approaches that fit the problem and explains the fit through the submitted work | Uses reasonable structures and approaches with minor inefficiencies or explanation gaps | Uses structures or approaches that partly work but are awkward, fragile, or weakly justified | Uses structures or approaches that interfere with the solution or cannot be explained | No meaningful structure or approach evidence submitted |
| Testing and observable evidence | CC3, CC4 | 15% | Provides clear tests and visible evidence such as traces, timing tables, charts, diagrams, or comparison tables that support correctness | Provides reasonable tests or evidence with minor gaps | Provides limited evidence, or evidence is weakly connected to correctness | Provides little evidence beyond claiming that the solution works | No testing or evidence submitted |
| Correctness, efficiency, and tradeoff awareness | CC4 | 15% | Compares correctness, efficiency, readability, or tradeoffs using evidence and appropriate introductory vocabulary | Provides a reasonable evaluation with minor gaps | Makes limited or unsupported claims about correctness, efficiency, or tradeoffs | Does not meaningfully evaluate whether the solution works or fits the problem | No evaluation submitted |
| Responsible AI/tool-use disclosure | CC5 | 10% | Clearly discloses AI/tool use, validates output, and identifies what the student accepted, changed, rejected, or verified | Discloses AI/tool use and provides basic validation or explanation | Discloses AI/tool use incompletely or provides weak validation | Relies on AI/tool output with little explanation or validation | No AI/tool-use statement when required |
| Submission completeness and reviewability | CC1, CC3 | 10% | Submission is organized, complete, named clearly, and easy to review | Submission is mostly complete and reviewable with minor organization issues | Submission is difficult to review or missing some required pieces | Submission is disorganized, incomplete, or prevents accurate assessment | No reviewable submission |

# Part 2 Rubric - Personalized Explanation Defense

**Weight:** 30% of final assessment

Part 2 evaluates whether the student can explain the submitted work. This part
is not designed as a gotcha. It verifies understanding and can raise the final
assessment score when students can credibly explain working or mostly working
solutions.

| Criterion | WIDS Alignment | Weight | 4 - Exceeds | 3 - Meets | 2 - Developing | 1 - Needs Improvement | 0 - Missing |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Walkthrough of submitted solution | CC1, CC3 | 25% | Explains the main solution flow accurately from input to output and references specific submitted work | Explains the main flow with minor gaps | Explains parts of the solution but misses important logic or sequence | Gives a vague explanation that is difficult to connect to the submitted work | Does not attempt or cannot explain the submitted work |
| Explanation of correctness and evidence | CC3, CC4 | 20% | Connects tests, traces, tables, charts, or examples to why the solution works | Explains basic correctness evidence with minor gaps | Identifies evidence but has difficulty connecting it to correctness | Makes unsupported claims that the solution works | Does not discuss correctness or evidence |
| Assumptions, edge cases, and limitations | CC1, CC4 | 20% | Identifies assumptions, edge cases, limitations, or failure conditions accurately | Identifies at least one meaningful assumption, edge case, or limitation | Identifies a weak or incomplete limitation with limited relevance | Struggles to identify where the solution may fail or be limited | Does not address assumptions, edge cases, or limitations |
| Tradeoff and efficiency explanation | CC2, CC4 | 20% | Explains tradeoffs among approach, data structure, readability, correctness, and efficiency using appropriate vocabulary | Provides a reasonable tradeoff or efficiency explanation with minor gaps | Gives a limited explanation that is only partly connected to the solution | Provides little meaningful tradeoff or efficiency explanation | Does not address tradeoffs or efficiency |
| AI/tool-use accountability, if applicable | CC5 | 15% | Clearly explains what AI/tooling contributed, how it was checked, and what the student changed or accepted | Explains AI/tool use and basic validation with minor gaps | Provides partial explanation of AI/tool use but weak validation | Cannot explain AI/tool contribution or validation clearly | No required AI/tool-use explanation provided |

If AI was not used, the instructor may redistribute the AI/tool-use
accountability weight across the other Part 2 criteria.

# Scoring Notes

- Functional submitted work should receive substantial credit when it solves or
  mostly solves the assigned task, even when the implementation is not elegant.
- Part 2 should verify understanding and ownership of submitted work, not punish
  the student for using approved support.
- Memorized or rehearsed explanation can still count when it accurately
  explains the submitted work.
- Higher scores require students to explain evidence, assumptions, tradeoffs,
  and AI/tool use with increasing independence and accuracy.
- If a student submits working but poorly structured code, score the working
  solution in Part 1 and use Part 2 to assess whether the student can identify
  weaknesses and possible improvements.

`MRS-AL_Master_Rubric_System.md`, but it is intentionally narrowed for the final
assessment.

- T1. Problem Framing and Algorithmic Analysis

- T3. Algorithm Implementation and Testing
- T4. Correctness, Efficiency, and Tradeoff Evaluation
- T5. Observable Algorithm Behavior and Communication Evidence
- T6. AI/Data Foundations and Responsible Tool Use

Excluded:

- Core ability rows C1-C6

The exclusion of CORE abilities is intentional for this final rubric so that
the final assessment remains focused on technical evidence and explanation of
submitted work.
