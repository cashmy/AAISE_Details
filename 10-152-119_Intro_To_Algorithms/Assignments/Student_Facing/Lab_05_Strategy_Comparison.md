# LAB 5 - STRATEGY COMPARISON

**Week 5 - Recursion, Iteration, and Strategy Patterns**

---

# Context

Many problems can be solved in more than one way.

In this lab, you will solve a small problem with two strategies and compare
correctness, readability, and growth behavior.

---

# Objective

Compare two algorithmic strategies for the same problem.

Your work should demonstrate:

- problem framing
- two valid approaches
- testing and evidence
- explanation of tradeoffs

---

# Task

Choose or use an instructor-assigned problem:

- compute a factorial or cumulative product
- sum nested or grouped values
- find a path through a simple decision tree
- solve a small coin-change or greedy-selection problem
- compare brute force and greedy selection for a small scheduling or shopping
  scenario

Implement or simulate two strategies for the same problem.

---

# Requirements

Your submission must include:

1. A short problem statement.
2. Two strategy descriptions before code.
3. Two implementations or clearly documented simulations.
4. At least `4` tests, including one edge case.
5. A trace, decision tree, or comparison table.
6. A recommendation explaining which strategy is better for this problem.
7. A note about when your preferred strategy might not be best.

---

# AI Use

You may use AI after you have framed the problem and described your first
strategy.

Allowed AI uses:

- ask AI to suggest a second strategy
- ask AI to critique your tradeoff explanation
- ask AI to help debug one implementation

If AI generates code, you must test it and explain the strategy in your own
words.

---

# Submission Requirements

Submit your files or written response as instructed.

Your submission should include:

- problem statement
- two strategy descriptions
- two implementations or simulations
- tests
- visible evidence
- recommendation
- AI-use note, if applicable

---

# Reflection / Explanation

Answer briefly:

> What made one strategy a better fit than the other for your problem?

If you used AI:

> Did AI suggest a strategy you would not have considered? How did you evaluate
> it?
