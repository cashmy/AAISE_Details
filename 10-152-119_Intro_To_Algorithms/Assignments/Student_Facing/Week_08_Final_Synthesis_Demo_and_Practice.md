# WEEK 8 - FINAL SYNTHESIS DEMO AND PRACTICE

**Week 8 - Tradeoffs, Explainability, and Final Assessment Preparation**

---

# Context

Week 8 brings the course together.

This activity is not a new full lab assignment. It is a final synthesis and
practice activity that helps you prepare for the two-part final assessment.

---

# Objective

Practice comparing approaches, explaining evidence, identifying limitations,
and accounting for AI use before completing the final assessment.

Your work should demonstrate:

- comparison of possible approaches
- explanation of evidence
- recognition of assumptions and limitations
- responsible thinking about AI-generated suggestions

---

# Activity Structure

Your instructor will guide a compact final synthesis demo.

After the demo, you may complete short practice prompts such as:

1. Which approach is easier to explain, and why?
2. Which approach may be more fair or useful, and what assumption does it make?
3. What evidence would you want before recommending one approach?
4. What edge case could make the chosen approach fail?
5. If AI suggested a third approach, what would you verify before trusting it?

The instructor may collect these responses as participation or use them as an
informal readiness check.

---

# Final Assessment Reminder

This course does not use a traditional capstone project.

The final has two parts:

```text
Part 1 - Applied Solution Set
Part 2 - Personalized Explanation Defense
```

In Part 1, you will complete bounded algorithmic tasks and submit solutions with
evidence.

In Part 2, you will answer questions about your own submitted work. The goal is
to verify understanding, not to trick you.

---

# AI Use

AI may be used only within the boundaries stated by the instructor.

If AI is used during practice or the final, you must be ready to explain:

- what AI helped with
- what you accepted, changed, or rejected
- how you verified the result
- what decisions remained yours

---

# Reflection / Explanation

Answer briefly:

> What kind of evidence helps you trust an algorithmic solution?

Optional:

> What is one question you could ask yourself before trusting an AI-generated
> algorithmic answer?
