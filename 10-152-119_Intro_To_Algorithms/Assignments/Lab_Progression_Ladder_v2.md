# Lab Progression Ladder v2

## Purpose

This artifact defines the lab progression for `10-152-119 Introduction to
Algorithms v2`.

The course should use consistent lab expectations across Labs 1-7. Week 8 is
reserved for final synthesis and the two-part final assessment rather than a
full additional graded lab.

Not every lab needs to be an equal-weight manual-versus-AI comparison. AI
should be used where it improves comparison, critique, assumption testing,
explanation, or refinement.

## AI Involvement Levels

Labs may use the following progression:

```text
Manual First -> AI-Assisted -> AI-Injected -> AI-Integrated
```

- Manual First: students frame the problem, reason through the approach, and
  attempt pseudocode, tracing, or implementation before AI use.
- AI-Assisted: students use AI for explanation, research, vocabulary,
  comparison, or critique.
- AI-Injected: students use AI for coding help or code revision, then justify,
  test, explain, and adapt the output.
- AI-Integrated: students use AI as a refraction-based collaborator inside a
  larger structured process. This level is optional for `119` and should only be
  used when the lab or final task has enough structure to preserve student
  authorship.

## Standard Lab Pattern

Most labs should include some version of:

1. Problem framing
2. Manual reasoning or pseudocode
3. Python implementation or simulation
4. Testing and evidence
5. Comparison or tradeoff explanation
6. AI-assisted critique, alternate solution, explanation, or code support when
   appropriate to the lab level

## Lab 1 - Precision and Correctness

Primary focus:

- algorithms as precise instructions
- inputs, outputs, constraints, and assumptions
- test cases and edge cases

Likely activity:

- Write instructions, pseudocode, or Python for a simple task.
- Have another person or AI interpret the instructions literally.
- Revise based on ambiguity or failure.

Visual/tangible evidence:

- Trace table, checklist, or before/after instruction comparison.

AI role:

- Optional comparison of human-written and AI-written instructions.
- Level: AI-Assisted.

## Lab 2 - Growth and Big-O Intuition

Primary focus:

- input growth
- timing
- constant, linear, quadratic, and logarithmic intuition

Likely activity:

- Run simple Python timing experiments with increasing input sizes.
- Compare observed timing with expected growth patterns.

Visual/tangible evidence:

- Timing table or simple chart.

AI role:

- Optional explanation critique: ask AI to explain the results, then verify.
- Level: AI-Assisted.

## Lab 3 - Data Structure Choice

Primary focus:

- lists, dictionaries, sets, stacks, queues
- representation and access patterns

Likely activity:

- Solve the same small problem using two different structures.
- Compare clarity, lookup behavior, and tradeoffs.

Visual/tangible evidence:

- Structure diagram or comparison table.

AI role:

- Optional alternate representation suggestion.
- Level: AI-Assisted.

## Lab 4 - Search and Sort Behavior

Primary focus:

- linear search
- binary search
- sorting preconditions and scale

Likely activity:

- Implement linear and binary search.
- Demonstrate binary search failure when data assumptions are violated.
- Visualize or trace a simple sort.

Visual/tangible evidence:

- Search trace, sorted/unsorted comparison, or step-by-step sort table.

AI role:

- Useful for generating an alternate search/sort implementation to inspect and
  critique.
- Level: AI-Injected when AI generates or revises code.

## Lab 5 - Strategy Comparison

Primary focus:

- recursion and iteration
- brute force
- divide and conquer
- greedy strategy

Likely activity:

- Solve a small problem with two strategies.
- Compare correctness, readability, and growth behavior.

Visual/tangible evidence:

- Recursive call trace, decision tree, or strategy comparison table.

AI role:

- Use AI to propose an alternative strategy only after the student frames an
  initial approach.
- Level: AI-Assisted or AI-Injected depending on whether code is generated.

## Lab 6 - Graph Traversal and Real-System Modeling

Primary focus:

- graphs
- BFS and DFS
- paths, networks, workflows, or grids

Likely activity:

- Model a small grid, route, workflow, or relationship network.
- Compare BFS and DFS traversal behavior.

Visual/tangible evidence:

- Graph diagram, traversal order, grid path, or adjacency-list drawing.

AI role:

- Optional explanation or critique of graph representation.
- Level: AI-Assisted.

## Lab 7 - Similarity, Clustering, Recommendation, or Hashing

Primary focus:

- AI/data bridge concepts
- similarity and distance
- grouping, ranking, identity, or integrity

Likely activity options:

- Compare items using a simple similarity or distance measure.
- Group simple 2D points into clusters.
- Build a tiny recommendation/ranking example.
- Demonstrate hashing for lookup or integrity checking.

Visual/tangible evidence:

- Scatter plot, cluster grouping, ranking table, hash comparison, or similarity
  matrix.

AI role:

- Useful as a comparison partner, but students must validate assumptions and
  explain the algorithmic idea.
- Level: AI-Assisted or selective AI-Injected.

## Week 8 - Final Synthesis Demo and Practice

Primary focus:

- synthesis across Labs 1-7
- final assessment preparation
- tradeoffs
- explainability
- responsible AI/tool use

Likely activity:

- Participate in a compact lecture-demo comparing two approaches to an applied
  problem.
- Practice explaining correctness, efficiency, readability, assumptions, risks,
  and AI/tool use.
- Prepare for the two-part final assessment.

Visual/tangible evidence:

- Formative comparison table, trace, chart, graph, or demonstration.

AI role:

- Optional critique or alternate approach during the demo/practice activity,
  with explicit validation and discussion of limitations.
- Level: AI-Assisted or AI-Injected. AI-Integrated may be previewed only if the
  final task is explicitly framed as a larger structured process.

Assessment note:

- Week 8 is not a full additional graded lab.
- The last two class days should be allocated to the final:
  - Final Part 1 - Applied Solution Set
  - Final Part 2 - Personalized Explanation Defense

## Grading Emphasis Across Labs

The repeated evaluation categories should be:

- problem framing
- implementation or simulation
- correctness and testing
- efficiency and tradeoff reasoning
- visual/tangible evidence
- explanation and communication
- AI accountability when AI is used
