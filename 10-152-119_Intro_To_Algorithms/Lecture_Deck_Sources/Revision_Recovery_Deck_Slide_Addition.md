# Revision Recovery Deck Slide Addition

**Reusable Course Slide Source**

---

# Purpose

This artifact provides a reusable first-week slide addition for courses that
allow students to recover learning through revision.

The intent is to communicate a mastery-learning policy without implying that
students can ignore first attempts or receive automatic high grades without
effort.

Use this slide when a course allows revised work to be regraded up to a defined
level, such as `3 - Meets`, after feedback, review, and demonstrated
improvement.

---

# Recommended Placement

Use during the first class meeting after the grading/rubric overview and before
the first major lab or assignment explanation.

This slide can also be reused before a major project or final review period if
students need a reminder that revision is part of professional learning.

# Slide Source

## Slide - Earn a B Through Revision

**Delivery Category:** Core / Course Policy

**Slide Text:**

Learning can recover your grade.

If you submit the original work on time, review the feedback, and revise the
work to show improved understanding, revised rubric elements may be regraded up
to:

```text
3 - Meets
```

Revision does not erase the learning path.

It proves that learning happened.

**Instructor Notes:**

Frame this as mastery learning and professional refactoring. Students should
understand that an early misunderstanding is not permanent failure if they
later demonstrate the concept correctly.

Be clear about the boundaries:

- the original submission must be on time
- the revision must address feedback or reviewed solutions
- the student must explain what changed and why
- the regrade cap is `3 - Meets`, not `4 - Exceeds`
- copied corrections without understanding do not qualify
- the policy may apply to labs or selected assignments, not necessarily every
  assessment unless the instructor says so

Use a simple example:

> If Lab 01 is weak because the first algorithm is vague, the student may revise
> it after review by making the inputs, thresholds, tests, and edge cases clear.
> If the revised work now satisfies the rubric element, that element can be
> regraded up to `3 - Meets`.

The point is not grade inflation. The point is to reward demonstrated learning,
revision, and ownership.

**Transition Cue:**

This course values the first attempt, but it values demonstrated learning even
more. Now look at what counts as evidence that learning actually happened.

**Optional Visual Notes:**

Show a simple before/after revision path:

```text
First attempt -> feedback -> revised evidence -> Meets
```

Avoid visuals that imply a free pass, shortcut, or guaranteed grade without
work.

# Alternate Title Options

Use the title that best fits the course culture:

- Earn a B Through Revision
- Learning Can Recover Your Grade
- Revision Can Demonstrate Mastery
- Refactoring Counts as Learning
- Feedback Is Part of the Grade Path

Avoid:

- Guaranteed B
- Easy B
- Free Retake
- No Penalty

Those titles may unintentionally suggest that first attempts do not matter.

---

# Suggested Policy Language

The following wording can be adapted for a syllabus, LMS page, or assignment
policy:

```text
Revision Recovery Policy

Students who submit the original work on time may revise selected assignments
after feedback, review, or class discussion. Revised rubric elements may be
regraded up to 3 - Meets when the revision demonstrates improved understanding.

To qualify, the revised submission must include a short explanation of what was
changed, why it was changed, and how the student verified the improvement.

Revision credit rewards demonstrated learning. It does not apply to copied
corrections without explanation, missing original submissions, or assessments
excluded by the instructor.
```

# Instructor Use Guidance

This policy works best when students understand that revision requires evidence.

Strong revision evidence may include:

- corrected code or pseudocode
- improved test table
- clearer assumptions
- edge-case correction
- revised README explanation
- short reflection on what changed
- AI-use note if AI helped during revision
  - What the AI contributed
  - Your evaluation of the AI's contribution
  - What you see as the potential benefits and limitations of the AI
    contribution

The instructor can apply the policy at the rubric-element level rather than
requiring the entire assignment to be regraded.

Example:

```text
Original T1 score: 1 - Needs Improvement
Revision shows clear problem statement, inputs, outputs, assumptions, and edge
cases.
Updated T1 score: 3 - Meets
```

This preserves the distinction between eventual competency and exceptional
first-pass or independently extended work.

# Reuse Notes

This slide is course-neutral.

Before reuse, adjust:

- assignment types covered by revision
- regrade cap
- due-date requirements
- AI-use boundaries
- whether the final assessment is excluded
- local LMS or institutional grading language

For high-stakes assessments, clarify whether revision recovery applies.
