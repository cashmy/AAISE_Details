# **Remote Class Participation Expectations**

---

## **Purpose**

This course is delivered fully online through live remote class meetings.

Because this is a live class, students are expected to participate as present,
visible, and engaged members of the learning environment.

Remote attendance is still attendance. It is not the same as watching a video
later or listening in the background while doing something else.

---

## **Core Expectation**

During live class meetings, you are expected to:

* join on time
* keep your camera on
* be visible and attentive
* participate when asked
* dress and present yourself appropriately for a class environment
* remain available for discussion, questions, demonstrations, and lab support

Keeping your camera off for the class session is not acceptable unless you have
received instructor approval or there is a temporary technical issue.

---

## **Camera Use**

Your camera should be on during live class.

This helps the instructor:

* confirm attendance and participation
* notice when students are confused or need support
* create a more interactive class environment
* support discussion, demonstration, and lab work

If you have a temporary issue with your camera, internet connection, or physical
space, communicate with the instructor as soon as possible.

---

## **Professional Presentation**

You do not need to dress formally for class.

You should, however, present yourself in a way that is appropriate for a live
learning environment.

This means:

* wear appropriate clothing
* be reasonably groomed
* avoid joining from bed if possible
* avoid distracting or inappropriate backgrounds
* avoid behavior that would not be appropriate in an in-person classroom

The goal is not perfection. The goal is to show that you are present, prepared,
and respectful of the class environment.

---

## **Learning Space**

Choose a location where you can participate as fully as possible.

When possible:

* sit upright at a desk or table
* keep course materials nearby
* reduce background noise
* use headphones or earbuds if needed
* keep your keyboard, editor, browser, and class materials available

If your home environment makes this difficult, communicate with the instructor.
The goal is to solve the problem early instead of disappearing from the class.

---

## **Microphone And Audio**

You may keep your microphone muted when you are not speaking.

You are still expected to respond when called on, participate in discussion, and
ask questions when you need help.

If audio is not working, use chat immediately so the instructor knows you are
still present and trying to participate.

---

## **Multitasking**

During class, avoid activities that pull you away from learning.

Examples include:

* working another job
* gaming
* watching unrelated videos
* leaving the computer for long periods
* driving during class
* completing unrelated homework

This course includes demonstrations and lab work that require attention. Missing
those moments will make later assignments much harder.

---

## **Pair Programming And Conflict Resolution**

Some course activities may involve pair programming or small-group work.

Working with another person is part of professional technical practice. It can
also create frustration, disagreement, or personality conflict.

If a normal work-related conflict occurs, use this process:

1. Start with a respectful direct conversation with the person involved.
2. Focus on the specific behavior, task, or work issue.
3. Give the other person a chance to clarify or respond.
4. Try to agree on a practical next step.
5. If the issue continues or cannot be resolved, contact the instructor.

Do not begin by complaining to other students, creating side conversations, or
gossiping about the person or problem. Address the issue directly first when it
is reasonable and safe to do so.

This does not mean you must handle serious problems alone.

If the concern involves harassment, discrimination, threatening behavior,
personal safety, or anything that feels unsafe, contact the instructor or the
appropriate college support immediately.

---

## **Communication If Something Goes Wrong**

Technology problems happen.

If you have a problem with Zoom, internet access, audio, video, or your learning
space, communicate with the instructor as soon as possible.

Use the communication method provided by your instructor.

Do not wait until the end of class or the end of the week to explain repeated
attendance or participation problems.

---

## **Privacy Or Accommodation Concerns**

If you have a privacy concern, disability accommodation, medical issue, or other
serious reason that affects camera use or remote participation, contact the
instructor as early as possible.

The expectation is active participation. If a specific adjustment is needed, it
should be discussed before it becomes a repeated class issue.

---

## **Simple Rule**

Treat the live Zoom meeting like a real classroom.

Be present. Be visible. Be prepared. Be respectful.
