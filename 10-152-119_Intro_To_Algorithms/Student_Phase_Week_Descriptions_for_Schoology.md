# Student Phase and Week Descriptions

**Course:** `10-152-119 Algorithmic Problem Solving`

---

# Purpose

This artifact provides short student-facing descriptions for the course phases
and weeks.

These descriptions are intended for Schoology, syllabus support, weekly
orientation, or module introductions.

They are not lesson notes. They are cognitive pre-framing cues that help
students understand what they are about to learn and why the difficulty makes
sense.

---

# Design Principle

Keep these descriptions:

- short
- directional
- reassuring
- human-friendly
- mostly non-technical

They should help students feel:

```text
This is the next stage.
This difficulty has a purpose.
I am learning how to think through solutions, not just memorize names.
```

# Part 1 - Unit / Phase Descriptions

### Unit (Phase) 1 - Algorithmic Foundations

In this phase, you will learn how to turn unclear problems into clear steps.
You will identify inputs, outputs, assumptions, and tests before worrying about
whether a solution is fast.

The goal is to understand what an algorithm is, how to check whether it works,
and why clear thinking matters before code or AI assistance can be trusted.

### Unit (Phase) 2 - Data Structures: Search, Sort, and Growth

In this phase, you will learn how the way data is organized affects what your
program can do. You will use data structures, search, and sorting to see why
some approaches work better as the amount of data grows.

The goal is to move from "I found a solution" toward "I understand why this
solution fits the data."

### Unit (Phase) 3 - Strategy and Behavior

In this phase, you will compare different ways to solve problems. You will use
traces, tables, diagrams, grids, and other evidence to see how algorithms
actually behave.

The goal is to understand that algorithms are choices. A solution can be
correct, but still have tradeoffs in clarity, speed, complexity, or fit.

### Unit (Phase) 4 - AI/Data Bridges

In this phase, you will connect algorithmic thinking to later work in AI, data
analytics, data modeling, and larger software systems. You will explore ideas
such as similarity, ranking, clustering, recommendation, hashing, and
explainability.

The goal is not to become an AI or data-science expert in this course. The goal
is to recognize the algorithmic ideas underneath those future topics and explain
them responsibly.

# Part 2 - Week Descriptions

### Week 1 - Algorithms, Precision, and Correctness

This week, you will learn what an algorithm is and why precise instructions
matter. You will define what a problem needs, what a solution should produce,
and how to check whether the result is correct.

The goal is to learn that unclear thinking can produce unreliable solutions,
even when the code appears to run.

### Week 2 - Big-O and Growth Intuition

This week, you will begin learning how solutions behave as the amount of data
grows. You will use timing, tables, or charts to compare simple examples and
make growth visible.

The goal is not advanced math. The goal is to understand why a solution that
works for a small input may not work well at a larger scale.

### Week 3 - Data Structures for Algorithmic Thinking

This week, you will learn how data structures shape the way a problem can be
solved. You will work with lists, dictionaries, sets, stacks, queues, trees, and
graphs at an introductory level.

The goal is to see data structures as choices that make some operations easier,
harder, faster, or clearer.

### Week 4 - Searching and Sorting

This week, you will work with classic search and sort problems. You will compare
simple approaches with more structured ones and learn why assumptions, such as
whether data is sorted, matter.

The goal is to connect correctness, preconditions, and performance in examples
you can trace and explain.

### Week 5 - Recursion, Iteration, and Strategy Patterns

This week, you will compare different problem-solving strategies. You will look
at recursion, iteration, brute force, divide-and-conquer, greedy choices, and
other strategy patterns at an introductory level.

The goal is to explain not only what works, but why one strategy may fit a
problem better than another.

### Week 6 - Graphs, Paths, and Models of Real Systems

This week, you will use graphs to model connected things such as routes,
workflows, relationships, grids, or networks. You will compare ways to move
through a graph and make traversal behavior visible.

The goal is to recognize that many real systems can be represented as connected
parts, and that the representation changes what questions are easy to answer.

### Week 7 - Similarity, Clustering, Recommendation, and Hashing

This week, you will see how algorithms can compare items, group records, rank
options, recommend choices, or create stable identities. These ideas help
prepare you for later AI, analytics, and data-modeling work.

The goal is to understand the basic algorithmic ideas underneath these systems
without turning this course into a machine-learning course.

### Week 8 - Tradeoffs, Explainability, and Final Assessment

This week, you will bring the course together. You will review how to compare
solutions, explain assumptions, identify tradeoffs, account for AI use, and
support your work with evidence.

The goal is to submit working algorithmic solutions and then explain your own
work clearly during the final assessment.

# Student-Facing Throughline

Across the course, you will move through this progression:

```text
define the problem clearly
-> test whether a solution works
-> choose useful data structures
-> compare solution strategies
-> observe algorithm behavior
-> connect algorithms to AI and data systems
-> explain tradeoffs responsibly
```

The most important outcome is algorithmic judgment: the ability to choose,
test, compare, and explain a solution strategy.
