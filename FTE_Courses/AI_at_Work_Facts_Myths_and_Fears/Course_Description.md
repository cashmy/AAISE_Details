# AI at Work: Facts, Myths, and Fears

**Alternate Title:**  
_Understanding Workplace AI, Employment Concerns, and the Changing Role of Employees_

## Course Description

This one- to two-hour continuing-education course provides a practical and candid introduction to workplace AI for employees who may be uncertain about its capabilities, limitations, or effect on their jobs.

Using examples relevant to industrial employers and the broader business community, the course separates common myths from realistic workplace changes. Participants examine the difference between jobs and tasks, where human judgment remains essential, and how employees can prepare to work responsibly with AI.

The course does not dismiss employment concerns or promise that every role will remain unchanged. Its purpose is to replace speculation with informed discussion, practical understanding, and an individual readiness plan.

## Target Audience

- Employees with limited AI experience
- Industrial, administrative, and office personnel
- Frontline employees and supervisors
- HR and workforce-development professionals
- Teams preparing for organizational AI adoption

No technical AI background is required.

## Flexible Course Structure: Approximately 1-2 Hours

1. **Why AI Creates Workplace Anxiety - 10-15 minutes**  
   Acknowledge common concerns, sources of uncertainty, and the difference between productive caution and unsupported assumptions.

2. **What Workplace AI Can and Cannot Do - 15-25 minutes**  
   Introduce generative AI, explain common limitations, and distinguish assistance, collaboration, and embedded AI features.

3. **Facts, Myths, and Misunderstandings - 15-25 minutes**  
   Examine claims about accuracy, intelligence, automation, job replacement, privacy, and the need for human review.

4. **Jobs, Tasks, and Changing Responsibilities - 15-25 minutes**  
   Explore how AI may affect parts of a job differently and why judgment, accountability, experience, communication, and contextual knowledge remain important.

5. **Personal AI Readiness - 10-20 minutes**  
   Identify one task to learn more about, one skill to strengthen, and one question to raise with an employer before using AI at work.

The one-hour version functions as a briefing with limited discussion. The two-hour version allows additional examples, facilitated discussion, and a personal readiness activity.

## Learning Outcomes

Participants will be able to:

- Describe workplace AI in practical, nontechnical language.
- Separate common AI myths from more realistic capabilities and risks.
- Distinguish between the automation of tasks and the elimination of an entire job.
- Identify responsibilities that continue to require human judgment and accountability.
- Develop constructive questions about AI use within their organization.
- Create a basic personal AI readiness plan.

## Product Approach

Familiar AI products and AI-enhanced business applications may be referenced to make the discussion concrete. The course remains vendor-neutral and does not teach product-specific features.

## Facilitation Principle

The discussion should be honest, respectful, and non-dismissive. Participants should not be told that AI cannot affect employment. Instead, the course should help them understand likely changes, recognize their continuing value, and identify practical ways to prepare.
