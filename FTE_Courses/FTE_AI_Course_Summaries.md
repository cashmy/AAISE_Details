# FTE AI Course Summary

This document summarizes the proposed continuing-education AI courses (for consideration) to date. Industrial employers are the primary context, while the courses remain applicable to the broader business community. All primary course titles meet the 42-character limit.

## Course Offerings

### Practical AI for Industrial HR

**Title length:** 30 characters  
**Alternate title:** _Using Generative AI to Improve Candidate Discovery, Resume Review, and Business Efficiency in Industrial Organizations_  
**Hours:** 3-4 hours; expanded 8-hour option

This practical workshop helps HR professionals, hiring managers, administrative staff, and operational leaders use AI assistance and collaboration to support candidate review, HR communications, and administrative efficiency while maintaining confidentiality, human oversight, and employer decision-making.

**Scheduling:** Standalone. It may be offered without a prerequisite because it includes an overview of workplace AI and prompting. For participants completing the larger series, it fits well after **AI Assistance and Better Prompting**.

---

### Building AI Policy and Governance

**Title length:** 33 characters  
**Alternate title:** _Developing Practical AI Policy, Security, and Governance Documents for Business_  
**Hours:** 2 hours

This workshop helps organizational leaders and staff examine acceptable AI use, information security, privacy, human oversight, accountability, and review practices while beginning a starter AI governance document for their organization.

**Scheduling:** Standalone. It is especially appropriate for leaders, HR, IT, security, and employees assigned to AI governance. It may be offered before organizational AI adoption or after participants have completed an introductory AI course.

---

### AI at Work: Facts, Myths, and Fears

**Title length:** 35 characters  
**Alternate title:** _Understanding Workplace AI, Employment Concerns, and the Changing Role of Employees_  
**Hours:** 1-2 hours

This introductory course addresses common workplace concerns about AI, including fear of job loss. Participants separate myths from realistic capabilities, consider how AI may change tasks and responsibilities, and develop a practical personal-readiness plan.

**Scheduling:** Standalone and recommended as the entry point for employees with limited AI experience or concerns about organizational AI adoption.

---

## Three Ways Businesses Use AI

**Series title length:** 28 characters  
**Alternate series title:** _Understanding AI-Assisted, AI-Enabled, and AI-Embedded Workplace Applications_  
**Total hours:** 7-10 hours across three standalone sessions

This series introduces three working classifications of workplace AI. Each session begins with a brief overview of all three classifications, so participants may take any session independently or complete the full series in the recommended order.

### AI Assistance and Better Prompting

**Title length:** 34 characters  
**Alternate title:** _Using Generative AI to Support Everyday Workplace Tasks_  
**Hours:** 3-4 hours

Participants learn to select appropriate tasks, write effective prompts, improve initial results, verify AI-generated content, and apply AI assistance to everyday workplace activities using fictional or nonconfidential information.

**Scheduling:** Standalone and recommended as the first skills-based session in the three-classification series.

### AI-Enabled Workplace Collaboration

**Title length:** 34 characters  
**Alternate title:** _Working Iteratively with AI on Workplace Tasks and Projects_  
**Hours:** 3-4 hours

Participants learn to work iteratively with AI across multiple stages of a task or project, including breaking work into stages, managing context, reviewing intermediate results, redirecting the process, and maintaining responsibility for the final work.

**Scheduling:** Standalone. It includes a concise prompting review, but it is recommended after **AI Assistance and Better Prompting** when participants are completing the full series.

### Using AI-Embedded Business Apps

**Title length:** 31 characters  
**Alternate title:** _Understanding and Evaluating AI Features in Workplace Applications_  
**Hours:** 1-2 hours

Participants learn to recognize AI features built into workplace applications and consider data access, permissions, security, accuracy, organizational approval, and human oversight. The course may follow an employee-use pathway, an organizational-evaluation pathway, or a combination of both.

**Scheduling:** Standalone. It is recommended as the third session in the classification series, but it may also be offered independently when an organization is considering or introducing AI-enhanced applications.

## Suggested Learning Order

### General Employee Pathway

1. **AI at Work: Facts, Myths, and Fears** - Establish awareness, address concerns, and build readiness.
2. **AI Assistance and Better Prompting** - Develop foundational, task-level AI skills.
3. **AI-Enabled Workplace Collaboration** - Extend those skills into iterative tasks and projects.
4. **Using AI-Embedded Business Apps** - Understand AI features within workplace applications.

### Applied HR Pathway

1. **AI at Work: Facts, Myths, and Fears** - Optional introductory session.
2. **AI Assistance and Better Prompting** - Recommended foundation.
3. **Practical AI for Industrial HR** - Apply AI assistance and collaboration to HR and hiring activities.

### Leadership and Governance Pathway

1. **AI at Work: Facts, Myths, and Fears** or **AI Assistance and Better Prompting** - Establish a shared understanding of workplace AI.
2. **Building AI Policy and Governance** - Begin the organization's governance and acceptable-use framework.
3. **Using AI-Embedded Business Apps** - Use the organizational-evaluation pathway when considering AI-enhanced applications.

## Standalone Availability

Every course may be delivered independently. The suggested sequences provide a progressive learning experience but are not prerequisites. Each course supplies the background needed for its stated objectives and uses vendor-neutral instruction with familiar business-product examples where helpful.
