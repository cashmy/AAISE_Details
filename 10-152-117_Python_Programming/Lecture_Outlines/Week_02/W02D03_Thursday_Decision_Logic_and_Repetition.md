# 5. Core Concepts (10-20 minutes)

## Concept 1 - Conditions can control repeated behavior

Plain-language explanation:

* a loop may repeat until a condition changes
* logic can decide whether the loop continues or ends

Why it matters:

* many small interactive programs use both ideas together

## Concept 2 - Validation is often a logic-plus-repetition pattern

Plain-language explanation:

* a program may keep asking or checking until the input is acceptable

Why it matters:

* this is a practical use case students can recognize immediately

## Concept 3 - Integration should stay small

Plain-language explanation:

* combining concepts does not mean making the program large

Why it matters:

* Assignment success depends on explainability, not ambition

## Concept 4 - Debugging combined logic means tracing step by step

Plain-language explanation:

* when logic and loops mix, students must still ask:
* what condition is being checked
* what repeats
* what changes

Why it matters:

* this prepares students for Week 4 debugging habits later

# 8. Common Mistakes / Watch-Fors (5-8 minutes)

## Mistake 1 - combining too much at once

Why it happens:

* students think integration means adding every feature they can imagine

Correction:

* keep examples bounded and practical

## Mistake 2 - the condition is checked, but nothing changes

Why it happens:

* students understand the logic but not the state update

Correction:

* ask exactly what changes after each loop cycle

## Mistake 3 - students can run it, but cannot explain it

Why it happens:

* the code may be copied or assembled without full reasoning

Correction:

* require verbal walkthroughs of the path, loop, and stopping condition

---

# 9. Explain / Checkpoint Questions (3-8 minutes)

Ask:

* Which condition controls this program?
* What repeats?
* What changes after one cycle?
* Why does the program stop?
* If the condition were different, what would happen?
