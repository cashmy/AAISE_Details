# 5. Core Concepts (10-20 minutes)

## Concept 1 - Constraints are part of the design, not the enemy of the design

Plain-language explanation:

* available time, course scope, and explainability all shape what should be built

Why it matters:

* capstone proposals must be realistic, not only interesting

## Concept 2 - Scope reduction is a professional skill

Plain-language explanation:

* removing features can improve the project

Why it matters:

* students often mistake reduced scope for failure

## Concept 3 - Structure should fit the project

Plain-language explanation:

* students should choose a likely project shape intentionally

Why it matters:

* proposal quality improves when structure is justified, not guessed

## Concept 4 - Risks and unknowns should be named early

Plain-language explanation:

* uncertainty around data, APIs, complexity, or time should be made explicit

Why it matters:

* named risks are easier to plan around than hidden risks

# 7. Hands-On / Lab Bridge (10-20 minutes)

Students should try:

* revising yesterday's framing artifact
* removing unnecessary features
* naming likely risks
* choosing a project structure
* drafting proposal-quality language

Suggested wording:

> "Your goal today is not to make the idea more exciting. Your goal is to make it more buildable, more explainable, and more likely to succeed."

Do not require yet:

* full build work
* advanced implementation detail
* complex feature planning beyond the course timeline

---

# 8. Common Mistakes / Watch-Fors (5-8 minutes)

## Mistake 1 - students protect feature count instead of protecting project clarity

Why it happens:

* bigger ideas can feel more impressive

Correction:

* reframe scope reduction as a quality decision

## Mistake 2 - structure choice is left vague

Why it happens:

* students may assume they can decide later without consequence

Correction:

* require a probable structure now, even if it later gets revised

## Mistake 3 - risks are unnamed because they feel inconvenient

Why it happens:

* students may avoid uncertainty to keep the proposal sounding strong

Correction:

* show that named risks improve approval quality rather than weaken it

---

# 9. Explain / Checkpoint Questions (3-8 minutes)

Ask:

* Which feature did you remove, and why?
* What structure fits your project best right now?
* What risk or unknown should be named before building?
* Why is the revised scope stronger than the earlier version?
