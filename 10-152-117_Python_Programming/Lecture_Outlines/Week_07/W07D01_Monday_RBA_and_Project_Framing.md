# 3. Opening Frame (2-4 minutes)

Today introduces a distinct development posture rather than a new Python syntax topic.

Key message:

* a project can be framed before code is generated
* intent matters before implementation
* AI is more useful when the human has already clarified purpose and boundaries

Suggested wording:

> "Today is not about replacing Python fundamentals. It is about improving project outcomes by deciding what you are building, why you are building it, and what boundaries matter before major generation begins."

# 5. Core Concepts (10-20 minutes)

## Concept 1 - Prompt-first and intent-first are not the same

Plain-language explanation:

* asking AI for code immediately is different from defining project intent first

Why it matters:

* students need to see why a weaker start often creates weaker outcomes

## Concept 2 - Project purpose should be explicit

Plain-language explanation:

* what the project is for should be stated before the build begins

Why it matters:

* Assignment 13 depends on purpose, inputs, outputs, and constraints being clear

## Concept 3 - Structure choices begin before code generation

Plain-language explanation:

* even at a small scale, students can choose whether a project is likely a simple script, a function-based tool, or something slightly more structured

Why it matters:

* structure is part of planning, not only revision

## Concept 4 - AI is a collaborator, not the owner of project meaning

Plain-language explanation:

* AI may help draft or compare options
* the human still defines intent and judges fit

Why it matters:

* this is the heart of bounded RBA use in the course

# 7. Hands-On / Lab Bridge (10-20 minutes)

Students should try:

* choosing a small project idea
* defining its purpose
* identifying inputs and outputs
* identifying constraints
* writing one or two AI-use boundaries

Suggested wording:

> "Your goal today is not to generate code. Your goal is to produce a framing artifact that makes later code generation or implementation more coherent."

Do not require yet:

* a full architecture document
* advanced terminology
* major implementation

---

# 8. Common Mistakes / Watch-Fors (5-8 minutes)

## Mistake 1 - students write a vague ambition statement instead of a framing document

Why it happens:

* project ideas often feel exciting before they feel concrete

Correction:

* require specific purpose, inputs, outputs, constraints, and likely structure

## Mistake 2 - RBA is treated as a philosophy lecture

Why it happens:

* the terminology can sound abstract if it is not tied to project decisions

Correction:

* keep every concept tied to a practical project-framing choice

## Mistake 3 - AI boundaries are left vague

Why it happens:

* students may say "AI can help" without defining where it should stop

Correction:

* require at least one concrete statement about what remains a human decision

---

# 9. Explain / Checkpoint Questions (3-8 minutes)

Ask:

* What is your project for?
* Who or what uses it?
* What inputs and outputs matter?
* Why is intent-first stronger than prompt-first in this case?
* What should AI not decide for you?

---

# 10. End-of-Class Success Check

> By the end of this session, students should be able to frame a small project in terms of purpose, inputs, outputs, constraints, and AI boundaries, and explain why that framing improves the quality of what comes next.
