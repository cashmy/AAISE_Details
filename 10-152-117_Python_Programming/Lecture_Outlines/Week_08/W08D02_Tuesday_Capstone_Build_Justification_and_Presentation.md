# 3. Opening Frame (2-4 minutes)

Today is about strengthening the project rather than merely extending it.

Key message:

* revision is part of quality
* reality contact often changes the best plan
* cutting or simplifying can be a professional improvement

Suggested wording:

> "Reality contact is not only when the code breaks. It is also when the project reveals what matters more, what matters less, and what should be revised or removed."

---

# 4. Course Positioning (2-4 minutes)

Students now know:

* what their project is supposed to do
* what behavior they want to validate

Today adds:

* revision after testing or friction
* coherence-focused refactoring
* stronger explanation of what changed and why

Suggested wording:

> "A project gets stronger when the student can revise it intelligently. Today is about improving the fit between the original plan and what the running project has revealed."

# 5. Core Concepts (10-20 minutes)

## Concept 1 - Reality contact reveals priorities

Plain-language explanation:

* once a project is running, students can see what matters most

Why it matters:

* revision should be guided by reality, not only by preference

## Concept 2 - Refactoring can improve coherence

Plain-language explanation:

* code may need cleanup so that the structure stays understandable

Why it matters:

* a finished but confusing project is weaker than a cleaner, smaller one

## Concept 3 - Scope reduction can be evidence of maturity

Plain-language explanation:

* removing features can make the capstone more explainable and more reliable

Why it matters:

* students often need explicit permission to reduce

## Concept 4 - Students should be able to justify revision

Plain-language explanation:

* what changed and why should be part of the student's explanation

Why it matters:

* revision is one of the strongest signals of actual project ownership

# 7. Hands-On / Lab Bridge (10-20 minutes)

Students should:

* continue implementation
* remove or simplify weak features if needed
* improve readability and flow
* record one meaningful revision and why it happened

Suggested wording:

> "Your goal today is not to make the project bigger. Your goal is to make the project stronger, clearer, and more defensible."

Do not require:

* major new features
* unnecessary polish before core behavior is stable
* presentation design before the project itself is coherent

---

# 8. Common Mistakes / Watch-Fors (5-8 minutes)

## Mistake 1 - students interpret simplification as failure

Why it happens:

* feature count is often mistaken for project quality

Correction:

* explicitly frame reduction as intelligent project management

## Mistake 2 - refactoring becomes cosmetic instead of structural

Why it happens:

* students may change names or spacing but not address clarity problems

Correction:

* keep refactoring tied to readability, behavior, and explanation

## Mistake 3 - students cannot explain why they made changes

Why it happens:

* changes may be reactive instead of reflective

Correction:

* require at least one clearly stated revision reason

---

# 9. Explain / Checkpoint Questions (3-8 minutes)

Ask:

* What changed in your project today?
* Why did you make that change?
* What did reality or testing reveal?
* How did the revision improve clarity, reliability, or finishability?

---

# 10. End-of-Class Success Check

> By the end of this session, students should be able to revise the project intentionally, explain what they changed, and show why that revision made the project more coherent or more realistic.
