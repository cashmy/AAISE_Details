# 3. Opening Frame (2-4 minutes)

Today begins the final build phase with expectations for quality and explainability.

Key message:

* the capstone must run
* the capstone must be understandable
* important behavior should be validated, not merely hoped for

Suggested wording:

> "This week is not only about finishing code. It is about finishing code that you can explain, defend, and show evidence for."

---

# 4. Course Positioning (2-4 minutes)

Students now know:

* how to frame a project
* how to reduce scope
* how to define AI boundaries

Today adds:

* validation evidence expectations
* build focus under approved scope
* stronger AI-use accountability during implementation

Suggested wording:

> "Your project is now approved. The next question is not just, 'Can you build it?' The next question is, 'Can you show that the important parts actually work?'"

---

# 5. Core Concepts (10-20 minutes)

## Concept 1 - Important behavior should be named

Plain-language explanation:

* students should identify what part of the project matters most

Why it matters:

* validation is stronger when it is tied to meaningful behavior

## Concept 2 - Validation evidence can be small but real

Plain-language explanation:

* expected-vs-actual checks, simple test cases, and run evidence can all count when used intentionally

Why it matters:

* students may think only large formal testing counts

## Concept 3 - AI support must remain accountable during build

Plain-language explanation:

* AI may help implementation, but the student still owns correctness and fit

Why it matters:

* capstone quality depends on judgment, not only acceleration

## Concept 4 - Build choices should stay inside approved scope

Plain-language explanation:

* adding many features now can damage finishability and explainability

Why it matters:

* end-stage feature creep is a high risk

# 7. Hands-On / Lab Bridge (10-20 minutes)

Students should:

* continue building the approved project
* identify one or two important behaviors to validate
* keep notes on AI use if AI is involved

Suggested wording:

> "Your goal today is not to add every extra feature you can think of. Your goal is to keep the build aligned to scope and begin collecting evidence that the important behavior works."

Do not require yet:

* polished presentation materials
* complete final explanation artifact
* expansion beyond approved scope

---

# 8. Common Mistakes / Watch-Fors (5-8 minutes)

## Mistake 1 - students build without deciding what counts as validated

Why it happens:

* finishing code feels more urgent than planning checks

Correction:

* require a small validation target before build momentum runs away

## Mistake 2 - feature creep returns after approval

Why it happens:

* build excitement makes new features feel tempting

Correction:

* keep returning to the approved project scope

## Mistake 3 - AI use becomes invisible

Why it happens:

* students may use AI repeatedly without recording decisions

Correction:

* prompt students to note what AI helped with and what they changed or rejected

---

# 9. Explain / Checkpoint Questions (3-8 minutes)

Ask:

* What is the most important behavior in your project?
* How will you know that it works?
* What evidence can you show for that behavior?
* If AI helped today, what still required your judgment?

---

# 10. End-of-Class Success Check

> By the end of this session, students should be able to continue capstone development while naming the behavior they need to validate, the evidence they can collect, and the boundaries that keep the build aligned to the approved plan.
