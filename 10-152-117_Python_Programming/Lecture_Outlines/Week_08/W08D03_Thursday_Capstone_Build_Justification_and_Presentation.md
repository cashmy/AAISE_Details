# 5. Core Concepts (10-20 minutes)

## Concept 1 - Presentation is explanation, not performance only

Plain-language explanation:

* students should tell the story of what they built and why

Why it matters:

* Assignment 16 evaluates ownership and clarity, not just visible functionality

## Concept 2 - Run instructions and demonstration clarity matter

Plain-language explanation:

* if the project cannot be demonstrated cleanly, evaluation becomes harder and weaker

Why it matters:

* professionalism includes making the artifact understandable to another person

## Concept 3 - AI use must be disclosed and justified

Plain-language explanation:

* students should explain where AI helped, what they changed or rejected, and what remained their decision

Why it matters:

* this is a core accountability goal of the course

## Concept 4 - Revision is part of the final story

Plain-language explanation:

* what changed because of friction, testing, or reality contact shows understanding

Why it matters:

* final explanation should include growth, not only the finished state

# 7. Hands-On / Lab Bridge (10-20 minutes)

Students should:

* prepare to demonstrate the project
* explain purpose, logic, and testing
* show or submit AI-use justification if required
* answer instructor questions about the final result

Suggested wording:

> "Your goal today is not to sound polished in a vague way. Your goal is to make your project understandable, demonstrable, and accountable."

Do not require:

* elaborate performance
* inflated claims
* technical explanation beyond what the student can actually defend

---

# 8. Common Mistakes / Watch-Fors (5-8 minutes)

## Mistake 1 - the demo runs, but the explanation is weak

Why it happens:

* students may assume visible output speaks for itself

Correction:

* require explanation of logic, testing, and design decisions

## Mistake 2 - AI use is described too vaguely

Why it happens:

* students may try to minimize or generalize the role of AI

Correction:

* ask for concrete contributions and concrete human decisions

## Mistake 3 - the project story skips revision and reality contact

Why it happens:

* students may think only the final version matters

Correction:

* ask what changed and why that mattered

---

# 9. Explain / Checkpoint Questions (3-8 minutes)

Ask:

* What does your project do?
* What problem or purpose does it address?
* What behavior did you validate?
* What did AI help with, if anything?
* What did you change or revise because of testing or friction?
