# ASSIGNMENT 6 — DEBUG AND EXPLAIN

**Week 4 — Debugging, Testing, and Reading Structured Code**

---

# Context

Code does not always work the first time.

Debugging is the process of finding out what went wrong, testing possible fixes, and explaining why the final solution works.

In this assignment, you will repair broken code and explain your debugging process.

---

# Objective

Debug a provided or approved Python program and explain the problem-solving process.

Your work should demonstrate:

* error identification
* tracing and testing based on evidence
* repair of broken logic or syntax
* explanation of the bug and the fix

---

# Task

You will receive intentionally broken code or use an approved broken example.

Your job is to:

* identify the problem or problems
* repair the code
* test the fix
* explain what changed and what evidence helped you decide

---

# Requirements

Your submission should:

* identify at least `2` issues, unless your instructor provides different requirements
* repair the code so it runs correctly
* use simple test cases or expected-vs-actual checks
* include at least one piece of debugging evidence
* explain what each issue was
* explain why your fix works

Debugging evidence may include:

* labeled print-debugging output
* a short expected-vs-actual comparison
* a traceback line or error message that helped locate the issue
* a short explanation of where the value or behavior first became wrong
* an instructor-approved pytest result or other testing output

You may be introduced to professional testing styles such as unit tests, end-to-end tests, and mocking. For this assignment, you are only required to use simple checks unless your instructor gives additional directions.

---

# AI Use

Bounded AI debugging assistance may be allowed only after you attempt a manual diagnosis.

If AI is allowed, you must record what you tried first, what AI suggested, and what you accepted, changed, or rejected.

---

# Submission Requirements

Submit as instructed:

* corrected `.py` file
* brief debugging notes or report
* test cases or expected-vs-actual examples
* debugging evidence used to support at least one fix

---

# Reflection / Explanation

Answer briefly:

> What was one bug you found, what evidence helped you identify it, and how did you know your fix worked?

If you used AI:

> What did you try before using AI, and what did AI help clarify?

# Success Criteria

To succeed on this assignment:

* you should identify real issues
* your repaired code should run correctly
* your tests, checks, or debugging evidence should support your fix
* your explanation should show how you reasoned through the problem

---

# Instructor Notes

This assignment is a strong place to reinforce process over speed.

Students may want to guess randomly. Push them toward observation, tracing, and expected-vs-actual reasoning.

This assignment can follow demos that show print-debugging, pytest, and professional testing vocabulary. Keep the grading center on diagnosis, repair, verification, and explanation. Pytest can be shown or allowed, but should not become the main barrier unless the instructor explicitly chooses to require it.

The key learning target is:

> a bug is information that can be investigated.
