## Proposed Structure

Nine regular assignments across Weeks 1-7, followed by the existing two-part individual final. Weeks 4 and 5 have two connected assignments; other regular weeks have one staged assignment. Assignment count does not increase contact time. Individual homework is an expected component, as clarified by the instructor, and may intentionally accommodate work beyond labs, particularly in Week 4. Exact amounts and submission windows remain to be specified; no state-required number of homework hours is asserted here.

Use connected sequences with independent entry points rather than a course-long student application. Weeks 1-2 and 3-4 share a support-ticket data context but start from supplied contracts/fixtures. Week 5 uses focused strategy tasks; Week 6 uses a graph model; Week 7 uses a reviewed learning-resource subset. The Learning Resource Navigator is the instructor reference app, not a required student capstone.

This proposal incorporates the [Scope Adjustment Review](119_Cohort_Readiness_and_Scope_Adjustment_Review_v1.md) and [Selective Paired Programming Plan](119_Selective_Paired_Programming_and_Remote_Lab_Plan_v1.md). Its demo IDs refer to the companion [Demo List](119_Demo_List_and_Reference_App_Milestones_v1.md). The [Reading Guide](119_Proposed_Reading_Selection_and_Daily_Guide_v1.md) supplies weekly conceptual preparation; optional readings are not hidden assignment prerequisites.

`DEM-*` references identify independent daily topic demos in equipment, donation, recurrence, campus, and music contexts. Separately scheduled `APP-*` Navigator demonstrations reinforce the week's learning; they do not replace daily instruction or create additional assignment requirements. Assignment IDs and collaboration modes are unchanged by that separation.

Paired assignments use the [student-facing collaboration/submission guide](../PreCourse_Setup/Paired_Programming_and_Assignment_Submission_Guide.md): a shared public assignment repository, driver/navigator switches about every 20 minutes, commit/push/pull handoffs, and a complete copy in each student's own class repository for Schoology submission. Partners rotate per assignment; nine students form three pairs and one active trio. Additional paired work outside lab uses independently scheduled Zoom sessions. Individual reflections do not require a hybrid label.

Formal peer review occurs once, for individual AL06 applications in Week 5. It is a separate presentation/code-review block with short Schoology reviews and its own grading entry, not a routine requirement attached to paired work or the final assessment. Ordinary questions and review during paired development remain part of collaboration, not extra discussion-board assignments. This revised direction takes precedence over the older selective pairing plan where they differ.

### AL01: Ticket Rules and Follow-Up

Week 1 production authority: [Finalized Week 1 objectives and assignment scope](119_Week_01_Objectives_and_Assignment_Scope_v1.md). It bounds AL01 to an open-ticket view and a follow-up selector, one supplied boundary defect, one issued policy change, one focused refactor, and compact evidence. The broader bullets below remain the course-level summary; they do not authorize additional required features.

Context: structured help-desk tickets with ID, status, category, age, and priority fields. Data is fictional teaching data; no actual student/personnel records are required.

Required work:

- Define inputs/outputs, accepted values, missing-field behavior, and rule priorities.
- Separate validation, decision/filter logic, and output responsibilities using clear functions or modules.
- Implement single-criterion and combined-criterion filtering with explicit match/nonmatch behavior.
- Diagnose a supplied defective rule, then apply an instructor-issued rule change without breaking unaffected cases.
- Test normal cases, exact boundaries, invalid records, conflicting rules, empty results, and preserved behavior using the packet's coverage checklist, not a fixed test count.

Evidence: executable tests, readable results, and brief individual README observations covering repair, changed/preserved behavior, learning, and assistance. No mandatory pseudocode, duplicate evidence table, or separate peer-question artifact.

Supply fixtures, optional full-English staged starter, and test support, not the completed instructor solution. Use paired development in Zoom breakout rooms, rotating roles and making repository handoffs. Each student submits the complete copied solution and their own README through their class repository. Keep the lighter entry scope for adjustment, not extra features. The current AL01 packet is aligned with this paired-development workflow.

### AL02: Lookup Workload Investigation

Context: querying ticket IDs from a supplied clean dataset. Reuse AL01's record shape, not its implementation as a compulsory dependency.

Required work:

- Implement a repeated list-scan baseline and dictionary/set-based alternative with equivalent results and a defined duplicate-ID policy.
- Define the counted operation and predict growth before measurement.
- Compare four practical input sizes and two query workloads, including hits/misses. Use fixed generated inputs, repeated timing, and separate construction, query, and end-to-end costs.
- Identify a confound or limitation and qualify a recommendation. An observed crossover is not required; explain what the collected data supports.
- Explain storage and expected average-case lookup tradeoffs without claiming universal constant-time behavior.

Evidence: parity tests, counts/timing table, experimental method, and individual workload recommendation. Supply data generation and timer mechanics; groups own algorithms, cases, and interpretations. Synthetic growth data is labeled separately from any real resource catalog. Optional extension: a more detailed crossover or memory investigation.

### AL03: Ticket Registry, Queue, and Undo

Context: a ticket registry with FIFO processing and one defined reversible action.

Required work:

- Compare a list representation with an ID-keyed dictionary for at least three meaningful operations.
- Use a set for a defined membership need and a deque for FIFO pending work.
- Implement a stack-backed undo of one specified completed state transition. Define which state is restored; do not build a general undo engine.
- Handle duplicates, empty states, updates, cancellation, and stale queue entries.
- State and check an invariant connecting the registry, pending work, and completed/undo state. Explain whether a filtered result is a snapshot or a live view.

Evidence: operation/structure table, action/state trace, tests for each failure category, a collaborative decision, and individual explanation of a contract or invariant.

Supply action parsing, a driver, and fresh fixtures so AL01/02 completion is not required. Groups own structure choices and operations. Optional extension: multiple-operation undo or a supplied priority-policy comparison.

### AL04: Search Contracts and Correctness

Context: ticket records searched by a declared field/key.

Required work:

- Implement linear search and binary search in Python.
- Use supplied modular key-selection/matching examples and function signatures to separate criteria from traversal. Keep one documented result contract; implement the assessed traversal logic independently.
- Define result semantics for absent values and duplicated search keys. A documented any-match policy is acceptable; a lower-bound implementation is not required.
- Show that binary search depends on an ordering matching its comparison key. Produce a selected unsorted/wrong-key failure example; a lucky result is not a valid contract.
- Test empty/singleton input, endpoints, absence, and duplicate-key behavior.

Evidence: two small readable traces, independent expected outcomes, and a concise precondition explanation. Supply record fixtures, a named callable-key interface, and focused equality/ordered-comparison examples; no benchmark suite or app shell. Binary comparison must support left/right ordering, not arbitrary eligibility. Individual implementation/tests/explanation may continue as planned homework; the Tuesday checkpoint is formative, not a hard deadline. Optional extension: first/last matching key.

### AL05: Filter, Sort, and Search Pipeline

Context: filtering and ordering ticket records, followed by a declared lookup operation. Reuse AL03 record conventions and AL04 interfaces where helpful.

Required work:

- Implement stable insertion sort using a record key; compare its results with `sorted()` on equivalent inputs.
- Test ordered/reverse/shuffled records and tied keys; record comparisons/moves on a small fixture.
- Compose filtering, ordering, and search through clear interfaces. Do not run ID binary search on an array ordered only by priority or age.
- Compare a bounded scan workflow with a sort/search workflow, accounting for setup and whether data is reused between queries.
- Apply a changed criterion, ordering key, or query workload, and demonstrate preserved behavior where required.

Evidence: sort trace/count table, pipeline sketch, tests, bounded workload comparison, and an individual revision explanation. Reuse AL04's key/search interfaces and AL02's measurement plumbing where available. Supply a tested filter or allow earlier filter reuse, so integration does not re-assess unrelated construction. Supply the benchmark harness and a recovery search implementation for students whose AL04 is incomplete. Recovery code is clearly identified and is not assessed as their own AL04 implementation.

Each student builds the sort/pipeline and performs the bounded investigation individually. Informal help may share ideas, but no designated peer-review session or discussion post is required. Individual completion may continue as homework. Use one issued change and compact evidence, not a second reporting system. Optional extension: merge sort or an additional search contract.

### AL06: Nested Data: Recursion and Iteration

Context: students choose one of four useful applications with comparable supplied nested data. Fundraiser Tracker totals donations across campaigns/teams; Storage Usage Analyzer totals file sizes across folders; Project Effort Estimator totals hours across tasks/subtasks; Inventory Counter totals quantities across locations/storage areas. No real filesystem access, date calculations, or extra features are required. The fundraiser fixture differs from the daily donation demo; none reproduces the Navigator milestone.

Required work:

- Identify the recursive base case and iterative processing state through the implementation and brief explanation; no formal pre-code document is required.
- Implement recursive aggregation and an explicit-stack iterative alternative over equivalent nested input.
- Verify equivalent results for empty, flat, nested, and mixed-depth fixtures; define numeric-value and malformed-input policy.
- Produce a call/state trace and explain time, storage, readability, and data-shape fit at an introductory level.

Evidence: tests, trace, and individual comparison. Supply fixtures and traversal interfaces, not both completed algorithms. Specify a bounded leaf/numeric/malformed-input policy rather than requiring a validation framework. Protect a flexible completion checkpoint for first recursion learning. Week 5 is lower-pressure consolidation: do not add mandatory features or long reports to occupy spare time. If an alternative flattens input, account for flattening work rather than treating it as free. Optional extension: a second aggregation operation. No full divide-and-conquer sort is added to this assignment.

Keep recursion/explicit-stack aggregation, test coverage, and difficulty comparable across all four choices. Use the optional full-English starter. Mix application choices within review pairs/trios where practical.

**One formal peer-review activity:** reserve a proposed Thursday block of 30 minutes for each member to demonstrate their application, share relevant code, and answer questions, followed by 10-15 minutes for short Schoology reviews. Each student reviews the other member, or both others in a trio. The template asks what worked well, what they asked/noticed and the response, and one suggested improvement/test. Give review its own grading entry focused on specific useful feedback; do not grade the same review participation again in AL06 or require every suggestion to be implemented. The form/rubric remain to be created. Final assessments do not add another formal peer review.

### AL07: Greedy Claims and Memoization Evidence

Context: two small strategy investigations. The methods within each investigation address the same problem; coin change and a recurrence are not presented as competing solutions to one task.

Required work:

- Investigation A: implement/complete greedy change-making and compare it against a supplied small-input exhaustive reference. State the objective and verify an issued counterexample or a student-found one.
- Define nonnegative integer targets, positive integer denominations, and unreachable outcomes. Keep exhaustive inputs bounded.
- Investigation B: modify a supplied nonnegative-integer Fibonacci recurrence with explicit memoization; verify unchanged outputs and compare before/after calls and cache entries.
- Start each measured run with an empty cache so earlier queries do not silently change the experiment. Explain repeated subproblems and the storage tradeoff.

Evidence: counterexample/reference table, correctness tests, call/cache comparison, a collaborative question/decision, and individual explanation of where each strategy is useful or limited.

Supply the exhaustive solver, recurrence scaffold, counters, and an issued counterexample when discovery would delay core work. Keep one memoization modification, a small call comparison and compact evidence. This is bounded practice, not an independent DP design unit. Spare time is not a reason to require extensions or clear unrelated backlog. Optional extension: memoized minimum-coin logic checked against the small reference.

### AL08: Access Network: Traversal and Restricted Routes

Context: a fictional access/workflow network with approximately 10-15 nodes, multiple paths, a cycle, and a disconnected region. Students define edge meaning and directionality; shortest-hop paths are meaningful within that model.

Required work:

- Individually justify a model and its omissions; validate references in its adjacency list.
- In the paired phase, implement BFS and iterative DFS; record frontier/visited behavior and explain cycle termination and introductory O(V+E) work.
- Implement BFS parent tracking and path reconstruction; test start=goal, unreachable goals, cycles, and valid endpoints.
- Individually apply an issued node/edge restriction, rerun tests, and explain why the returned path remains valid or why none exists.

Evidence: graph diagram/text representation, small traversal traces, tests, and an individual restriction response. Specify neighbor order for reproducibility without declaring one DFS order universally correct.

Groups use a common supplied fixture for traversal implementation after individual modeling. Pilot self-selected partners with instructor-supported trio placement, rotating roles, individual checks, and regrouping when needed. Positive working relationships may improve effectiveness; no fixed time reduction is assumed. Bound model choices, reuse BFS parent state, and supply surrounding driver/interfaces while keeping assessed traversal/path logic student-owned. Each student submits the identified shared baseline plus their own restriction code/evidence; no claim of individual authorship of the whole shared implementation. Weighted shortest-path algorithms remain optional.

### AL09: Explainable Resource Ranking and Integrity

Context: an assigned real learning-resource subset/profile, transferring concepts from the daily music demos. Use a different subset/profile from the separate Navigator milestone demonstration. Students solve a bounded scoring task; they do not reproduce the full reference app.

Required work:

- Represent topics/tags and implement shared-tag count plus Jaccard similarity with an explicit empty/missing-data policy.
- Implement deterministic top-k ranking and explain matched features and tie handling. Hand-check selected scores.
- Add one bounded weighted-feature revision. Define the formula, defaults, permitted nonnegative weights, and zero-total behavior; do not conflate raw weighted count with normalized similarity.
- Compare baseline/weighted ranks and demonstrate a concrete effect of a profile change, missing data, or catalog coverage. Do not claim a mathematically correct score guarantees a good recommendation.
- Use a supplied canonical serialization helper and library digest to compare a local catalog snapshot before/after one edit. Distinguish integrity evidence from authenticity, fairness, and source correctness.

Evidence: score/rank table, matched-feature explanations, tests for ties/empty inputs/missing tags/self-match policy, changed-ranking example, digest comparison, and individual limitations/tool-use account.

Supply reviewed metadata, small hand-checkable fixtures, and the integrity helper. Instructor-assigned study time, difficulty, and prerequisites remain identifiable as judgments. Students need not research dozens of URLs during algorithm lab time. Optional extension: clustering or a numeric distance experiment.
