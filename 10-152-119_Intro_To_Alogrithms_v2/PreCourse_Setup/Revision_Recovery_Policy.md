# Revision Recovery Policy

**Reusable Pre-Course Artifact**

---

# Purpose

This policy explains how revision can support learning, mastery, and fair
assessment.

The goal is simple:

Students should not be permanently punished for an early misunderstanding if
they later demonstrate that they learned the concept, improved the work, and
can explain what changed.

Revision recovery rewards demonstrated learning. It is not a free pass, a
replacement for original effort, or a way to copy corrected answers without
understanding them.

---

# Core Principle

Learning is allowed to recover your grade.

If you submit the original work on time, review the feedback, and revise the
work to show improved understanding, revised rubric elements may be regraded up
to:

```text
3 - Meets
```

The original attempt still matters.

The revision shows what you learned after feedback, review, discussion, or
additional practice.

---

# What This Means

If an assignment is eligible for revision recovery, the instructor may regrade
specific rubric elements after revision.

Example:

```text
Original T1 score: 1 - Needs Improvement

The original submission did not clearly define the problem, inputs, outputs,
assumptions, or edge cases.

Revision:
The student rewrites the problem statement, lists inputs and outputs, explains
assumptions, adds edge cases, and updates the README.

Updated T1 score: 3 - Meets
```

The regrade happens at the rubric-element level. The whole assignment does not
need to be regraded if only one or two elements were revised.

---

# Why The Cap Is Usually 3 - Meets

The normal revision recovery cap is `3 - Meets`.

This protects the difference between:

- eventually demonstrating the required competency
- demonstrating exceptional, independent, or extended performance

A first submission or independently extended solution may still earn
`4 - Exceeds`.

Revision recovery is meant to confirm that the student now meets the expected
level of competency after feedback and revision.

---

# Eligibility Requirements

To qualify for revision recovery, the student must normally:

1. Submit the original work by the due date.
2. Review feedback, class discussion, posted examples, or instructor guidance.
3. Revise the work meaningfully.
4. Include a short revision note.
5. Explain what changed and why.
6. Show evidence that the revision now works or better satisfies the rubric.

Late, missing, copied, or unexplained corrections may not qualify.

The instructor may limit which assignments or assessments are eligible.

---

# Required Revision Evidence

A revision submission should include evidence of learning.

Strong revision evidence may include:

- corrected code or pseudocode
- improved test table
- clearer assumptions
- edge-case correction
- revised README explanation
- short reflection on what changed
- comparison between original and revised logic
- AI-use note if AI helped during revision
  - what the AI contributed
  - your evaluation of the AI's contribution
  - what you see as the potential benefits and limitations of the AI
    contribution

The revision note should be short but specific.

Useful pattern:

```text
What I changed:

Why I changed it:

How I verified the improvement:
```

---

# AI Use During Revision

AI may be used during revision only within the boundaries stated by the
instructor.

If AI helps with the revision, the student must explain:

- what AI helped with
- what AI suggested
- what the student accepted, changed, or rejected
- how the student tested or verified the result
- what the student still understands and owns

AI can support learning, but it does not replace the student's responsibility
to understand the revised work.

---

# What Does Not Qualify

Revision recovery does not normally apply when:

- the original assignment was not submitted
- the revision is copied without explanation
- the student cannot explain the revised work
- the revision does not address the feedback or rubric issue
- the assignment or assessment was excluded by the instructor
- the student uses AI or another source without required disclosure

The instructor may also deny revision recovery when academic integrity concerns
prevent meaningful assessment of student learning.

---

# Adaptive Learning Requests

Some students may need an adaptation or accommodation in order to demonstrate
what they know.

Examples may include:

- verbal explanation instead of a written explanation
- video or audio submission
- interpreter or translation support
- extended time, when approved
- alternate demonstration format
- assistive technology
- modified presentation format

Students who need an academic adjustment, auxiliary aid, or accommodation
should contact the appropriate college accessibility, disability services, or
student support office according to institutional policy.

Students may also tell the instructor that they would like to discuss how to
demonstrate learning in an accessible format. The instructor may help direct
the student to the proper institutional process.

---

# Adaptation And The 3 - Meets Cap

The normal revision recovery cap is `3 - Meets`.

However, when an approved adaptation or accommodation changes the way a student
demonstrates learning, the instructor may remove or modify the `3 - Meets` cap
for the affected rubric element.

This should be done only when the cap would prevent the student from having an
equivalent opportunity to demonstrate mastery.

If the adaptation allows the student to demonstrate performance that genuinely
meets the `4 - Exceeds` description, the student may be graded objectively at
that level.

The purpose is not to give an automatic advantage. The purpose is to evaluate
the student's demonstrated learning fairly through an appropriate format.

---

# Instructor Notes

This policy should be applied consistently and documented clearly.

Recommended instructor practice:

- state which assignments are eligible for revision recovery
- state the normal regrade cap
- state revision deadlines
- require a revision note
- preserve original and revised evidence when practical
- coordinate formal accommodation/adaptation questions through the appropriate
  college office
- avoid making disability-related documentation decisions inside the course
  unless institutional policy assigns that role to the instructor

This policy does not replace institutional accommodation procedures.

---

# Course Reuse Checklist

Before using this policy in another course, adjust:

- assignment types covered
- revision deadlines
- regrade cap
- whether final assessments are excluded
- AI-use rules
- LMS submission steps
- local accessibility/disability services language
- instructor and institutional grading policies

---

# Student-Facing Summary

Revision recovery means:

```text
Try honestly.
Use feedback.
Revise meaningfully.
Explain what changed.
Show evidence.
Demonstrate learning.
```

The goal is not perfection on the first attempt.

The goal is learning that becomes visible.
