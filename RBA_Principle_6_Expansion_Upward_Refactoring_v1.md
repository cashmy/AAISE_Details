# RBA Principle 6 Expansion

## Bounded Vertical Oscillation and the Legitimacy of Upward Refactoring

This artifact expands Principle 6 from
[RBA_Principle_Set_v1.0.md](D:\@Artifact_Generation\108_AAISE_Details\RBA_Principle_Set_v1.0.md):

> Bounded Vertical Oscillation (ECL-Bounce)

The original principle correctly establishes that movement between layers is not
strictly linear and that controlled upward and downward motion is necessary to
resolve ambiguity and respond to real-world constraints.

This expansion clarifies a stronger implication that surfaced through applied
RBA work:

lower-layer contact does not merely refine downstream execution.

It can also reveal that the higher-order frame itself is incomplete, distorted,
or constrained by assumptions that should no longer govern the work.

---

## The Expansion

Bounded Vertical Oscillation should be understood to include not only:

- downward refinement from intent into structure and implementation
- upward clarification when ambiguity appears

but also:

- upward refactoring of the governing frame when lower-layer artifact contact
  reveals hidden constraints, false assumptions, or better structural
  understanding

In other words:

the system must permit reality discovered below to revise understanding above,
provided that the revision remains governed and does not dissolve architectural
coherence.

---

## Why This Matters

Many planning and delivery methodologies unintentionally produce what can be
called **incorrect constraints**.

These are constraints that feel official because they were established early,
but were actually formed under incomplete understanding.

When a process over-protects its initial definition, those early assumptions can
become more powerful than reality itself.

This creates a failure pattern in which:

- early framing is treated as fixed truth
- downstream discoveries are treated as inconvenience or scope drift
- the project continues under increasingly inaccurate constraints
- coherence degrades because the frame is no longer reality-aligned

RBA rejects that pattern.

Early framing is necessary, but it is not sacred.

If lower-layer artifact work reveals that the original frame is no longer
sufficient, the frame must be allowed to evolve upward rather than forcing
reality to conform to outdated assumptions.

---

## Distinction From Scope Drift

This expansion does **not** mean that every downstream idea should be allowed to
rewrite the governing layer.

The process remains bounded.

The point is not unrestricted revision.

The point is governed upward refactoring when there is legitimate evidence that:

- the original frame omitted an important reality condition
- the original structure created false limitations
- the system is now being governed by assumptions that are no longer valid
- lower-layer artifact contact has exposed a more coherent architectural reading

This is not drift.

It is correction in service of coherence.

---

## Human and Methodological Relevance

This expansion also helps explain why many familiar delivery models break down
in complex or rapidly changing work.

### Traditional PM and micro-waterfall patterns

These often overemphasize early definition and treat later discoveries as
deviation rather than valid feedback into the frame.

This can produce stability at the cost of truth.

### Agile patterns

Agile is more flexible, but it can still fail here when:

- the backlog preserves outdated assumptions
- sprint execution outruns reframing
- local iteration is allowed, but upward architectural revision is not

In these cases, even agile work can become trapped inside an obsolete frame.

RBA's bounded oscillation prevents that by recognizing that downward work is not
only execution.

It is also a discovery engine.

And discovery must sometimes refactor the layers above it.

---

## Applied Interpretation

A good practical reading of Principle 6 is now:

**Descent into lower layers is allowed to surface evidence that may require
upward revision of higher-layer framing. That revision is legitimate when it
improves coherence and corrects false or incomplete constraints without
collapsing governance.**

This preserves two things at once:

- architectural integrity
- evolutionary accuracy

Without the first, the system drifts.

Without the second, the system calcifies around its own early mistakes.

---

## Proposed Sub-Principle

If useful, this expansion can be named as a sub-principle:

### 6a. Upward Refactoring Legitimacy

Lower-layer discoveries are allowed to revise higher-layer framing when reality
contact reveals that the original frame is incomplete, distorted, or
prematurely closed.

Such revision must remain bounded by coherence and human governance, but it must
not be blocked merely because the earlier frame was established first.

---

## Working Summary

Principle 6 should now be read more strongly:

Bounded Vertical Oscillation is not only a permission to move up and down.

It is also a protection against false finality in early framing.

Its deeper role is to ensure that:

- reality discovered below can correct assumptions above
- architectural coherence is preserved during that correction
- projects do not become trapped inside their own incorrect constraints

That makes ECL-Bounce not merely a flexibility mechanism, but a core safeguard
against premature closure in complex design work.
