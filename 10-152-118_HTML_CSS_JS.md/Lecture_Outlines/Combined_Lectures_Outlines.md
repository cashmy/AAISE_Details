# 🧭 **WEEK 1 — MONDAY LECTURE OUTLINE**

**Title:** *Week 1 — Structure: Creating Something That Exists*

---

## **1. Opening Frame**

> “This week, we are not trying to build something impressive.
> We are trying to build something that exists—and works.”

* Introduce idea: existence before perfection
* Reduce pressure immediately

---

## **2. Course Positioning**

> “Over this course, you will build a system step-by-step.
> This week is the foundation—structure.”

* HTML = structure
* CSS = appearance (later)
* JS = behavior (later)

👉 Establish separation early (important for your philosophy)

---

## **3. Core Concepts**

* What a web page actually is (document, not “app” yet)
* HTML as structure (not styling)
* Elements and hierarchy (headings, paragraphs, lists)
* Multi-page thinking (not just one page)
* Navigation as connection between pages

---

## **4. Demonstration / Example Layer**

* Show:

  * simple HTML page (unstyled)
  * same page with clear structure vs messy structure

* Show:

  * multiple pages connected via links

👉 Emphasize:

* “this works” vs “this looks good”

---

## **5. Common Mistakes / Watch-Fors**

* Trying to make it look good (premature CSS thinking)
* Putting everything in one page
* Broken links (file names don’t match)
* Ignoring structure (just dumping content)

---

## **6. Tuesday Lab Bridge**

> “In lab, your goal is not design.
> Your goal is to create a working multi-page structure.”

Focus them on:

* pages exist
* navigation works
* content is organized

---

## **7. Closing Frame**

> “If your site works—even if it’s ugly—you succeeded this week.”

---

* Structure communicates intent

# 🧱 **PART 1 — MONDAY LECTURE TEMPLATE**

**Purpose:** Introduce the week, establish the mental model, prepare for Iteration 1 (Tuesday Lab)

---

## **Lecture Title**

> Example: *Week 3 — Layout & Responsive Thinking*

---

## **1. Opening Frame (1–2 minutes)**

**Goal:** Orient the student immediately

* What are we doing this week?
* Why does this matter?

**Template:**

> “This week, we are moving from ______ to ______.”

---

## **2. Course Positioning (2–3 minutes)**

**Goal:** Anchor in the progression (VERY important for your system)

* What have they already learned?
* How does this build on it?

**Template:**

> “So far, you’ve worked with ______.
> Now we are adding ______.”

---

## **3. Core Concepts (Primary Section — 10–20 minutes)**

**Goal:** Deliver the essential ideas (NOT everything)

List **3–5 concepts max**:

* Concept 1
* Concept 2
* Concept 3
* (Optional) Concept 4–5

**Guideline:**

* If it doesn’t support this week’s assignment → don’t include it

---

## **4. Demonstration / Example Layer (5–15 minutes)**

**Goal:** Make concepts visible

Describe what you will show:

* Example 1:
* Example 2:

**Guideline:**

* Show contrast (wrong vs right)
* Keep examples small and focused

---

## **5. Common Mistakes / Watch-Fors (3–5 minutes)**

**Goal:** Preempt frustration

* Mistake 1
* Mistake 2
* Mistake 3

**Template:**

> “You will likely see this when ______.”

---

## **6. Tuesday Lab Bridge (2–3 minutes)**

**Goal:** Align expectations for Iteration 1

* What students should focus on
* What *not* to worry about yet

**Template:**

> “In lab, your goal is not ______.
> Your goal is to ______.”

---

## **7. Closing Frame (1 minute)**

**Goal:** Reinforce clarity

> “This week is about ______.”

---

# 🧱 **PART 2 — WEDNESDAY CONCEPT FOCUS TEMPLATE**

**Purpose:** Deepen understanding, correct misconceptions, prepare for Iteration 2 (Thursday Lab)

---

## **Lecture Title**

> Example: *Week 3 — Layout Thinking & Responsiveness*

---

## **1. Re-Anchor the Week (1–2 minutes)**

**Goal:** Reconnect to the main idea

> “This week is really about ______.”

---

## **2. Deep Concept Focus (10–15 minutes)**

**Goal:** Strengthen mental model (not new content overload)

* Key Idea 1
* Key Idea 2
* Key Idea 3

**Guideline:**

* Explain *why*, not just *how*

---

## **3. Common Confusion Patterns (5–10 minutes)**

**Goal:** Address what students struggle with

* Confusion 1
* Confusion 2
* Confusion 3

**Template:**

> “A common issue is ______ because ______.”

---

## **4. Thinking Tools / Heuristics (5–10 minutes)**

**Goal:** Give them ways to reason independently

* Heuristic 1
* Heuristic 2
* Heuristic 3

---

**Goal:** Direct Iteration 2

**Template:**

**Goal:** Stabilize understanding
