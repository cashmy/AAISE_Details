# 🧱 Unit & Week Descriptions

---

## 🧭 What You’re Actually Doing (Important)

By adding descriptions, you are:

> **Installing the mental model *before* the experience happens**

That is high leverage.

Without this, students experience:

* “Why are we doing this?”
* “This feels random”
* “This suddenly got hard”

With this, they experience:

* “Oh… this is the next stage”
* “This difficulty makes sense”
* “I’m progressing, not failing”

---

## 🔑 Design Principle for These Descriptions

Keep them:

* **Short**
* **Directional**
* **Reassuring**
* **Non-technical (mostly)**

They are NOT:

* lesson content
* detailed instructions
* academic definitions

They are:

> **Cognitive pre-framing cues**

---

# 🧱 PART 1 — UNIT (PHASE) DESCRIPTIONS

These should feel like **chapter openers**.

---

## 🔹 Unit 1 — Foundations (Blue)

> In this phase, you will learn how web pages are built and how they are styled.
> You will focus on creating structure with HTML and controlling appearance with CSS.
>
> The goal is to build a strong foundation so you understand what your page is and how it is displayed—before adding behavior.

---

## 🔹 Unit 2 — Behavior (Yellow)

> In this phase, you will begin programming with JavaScript.
> You will learn how to make your pages respond to user actions and connect behavior to what you have already built.
>
> You will also begin learning how to debug when things break—a key skill for all developers.

---

## 🔹 Unit 3 — System Thinking (Green)

> In this phase, you will move beyond individual features and begin working with complete systems.
> You will learn how data flows, how applications manage state, and how different parts of a system interact over time.
>
> The focus shifts from “making things work” to understanding how systems behave.

---

## 🔹 Unit 4 — System Integrity (Purple)

> In this phase, you will improve and refine what you have built.
> You will focus on performance, usability, and reliability, and learn how to make systems more efficient and resilient.
>
> The goal is to move from working solutions to well-designed solutions.

---

## 🔹 Unit 5 — Capstone (Orange)

> In this phase, you will apply everything you have learned to a project of your choosing.
> You will design, build, and present a complete web solution.
>
> The focus is on independence, problem-solving, and clearly explaining how your system works.

---

# 🧱 PART 2 — WEEK DESCRIPTIONS

These should feel like:

> **“What am I about to learn—and why does it matter?”**

---

## 🔹 Week 1 — HTML: “Something Exists”

> This week, you will learn how to create the structure of a web page.
> You will build your first multi-page site and understand how content is organized and connected.
>
> The goal is to create something that exists and can be viewed in a browser.

---

## 🔹 Week 2 — CSS: “I Can Control Appearance”

> This week, you will learn how to style your web pages using CSS.
> You will control colors, spacing, and typography to make your content readable and visually consistent.
>
> The goal is to move from raw structure to intentional design.

---

## 🔹 Week 3 — Layout: “Control Space”

> This week, you will learn how to control layout and positioning.
> You will use tools like Flexbox and media queries to organize content and adjust for different screen sizes.
>
> The goal is to create layouts that work across devices.

---

## 🔹 Week 4 — JavaScript: “This Is Programming”

> This week, you will begin learning JavaScript as a programming language.
> You will work with variables, logic, and functions to understand how programs behave.
>
> The goal is to develop a programming mindset before connecting it to your web pages.

---

## 🔹 Week 5 — DOM: “Now It Connects”

> This week, you will connect JavaScript to your web pages.
> You will learn how to respond to user actions and update content dynamically.
>
> The goal is to make your pages interactive.

---

## 🔹 Week 6 — Debugging: “Things Break — And I Can Fix Them”

> This week, you will focus on debugging.
> You will learn how to identify problems, use tools to investigate them, and fix issues across HTML, CSS, and JavaScript.
>
> The goal is to build confidence when things don’t work.

---

## 🔹 Week 7 — Structured Behavior

> This week, you will improve how your code is organized.
> You will use functions and structure your logic so your programs are easier to understand and maintain.
>
> The goal is to move from “working code” to “well-structured code.”

---

## 🔹 Week 8 — Async: “Time Matters”

> This week, you will learn how programs handle time and delayed actions.
> You will begin working with asynchronous behavior and understand that not everything happens instantly.
>
> The goal is to think beyond step-by-step execution.

---

## 🔹 Week 9 — Modular Thinking

> This week, you will organize your code into smaller, manageable pieces.
> You will learn how to separate responsibilities and structure your code more effectively.
>
> The goal is to build systems that are easier to scale and maintain.

---

## 🔹 Week 10 — Data: “Beyond the Page”

> This week, you will work with data from outside your application.
> You will learn how to fetch data and display it dynamically.
>
> The goal is to connect your application to real-world information.

---

## 🔹 Week 11 — State: “Things Persist”

> This week, you will learn how applications remember and manage information over time.
> You will build features that maintain state and update consistently.
>
> The goal is to create applications that evolve and respond over time.

---

## 🔹 Week 12 — Performance

> This week, you will focus on improving how your applications run.
> You will learn techniques to make interactions faster and more efficient.
>
> The goal is to make your systems perform better.

---

## 🔹 Week 13 — Security

> This week, you will learn basic concepts related to application security.
> You will identify common risks and understand how to avoid unsafe patterns.
>
> The goal is to build safer, more reliable applications.

---

## 🔹 Week 14 — UX & Refinement

> This week, you will improve the user experience of your applications.
> You will refine layout, interactions, and visual feedback.
>
> The goal is to make your applications more intuitive and polished.

---

## 🔹 Week 15 — Capstone Preparation

> This week, you will plan your final project.
> You will define your idea, outline your approach, and begin building your solution.
>
> The goal is to prepare for independent development.

---

## 🔹 Week 16 — Capstone Build

> This week, you will focus on building your final project.
> You will apply everything you have learned and work through challenges independently.
>
> The goal is to complete a working system.

---

## 🔹 Week 17 — Capstone Presentation

> This week, you will present your project.
> You will explain how your system works and demonstrate your solution.
>
> The goal is to communicate your understanding clearly.
