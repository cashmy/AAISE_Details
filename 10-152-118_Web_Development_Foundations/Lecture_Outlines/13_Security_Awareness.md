# 🧭 **WEEK 13 — MONDAY LECTURE OUTLINE**

**Title:** *Week 13 — Security & Reliability: Building Safely*

---

## **1. Opening Frame**

> “Up to this point, you’ve focused on making your system work.
> This week, we focus on making sure it works under different conditions.”

* Shift from ideal use → real-world use

---

## **2. Course Positioning**

> “Before your capstone, your system needs to handle unexpected situations.”

* connects to:

  * Week 6 (debugging)
  * Week 9 (input)
  * Week 12 (stability)

---

## **3. Core Concepts**

* User input cannot always be trusted
* Input validation (basic level)
* Preventing unexpected behavior
* Handling missing or incorrect data
* Defensive programming (anticipating issues)

---

## **4. Demonstration / Example Layer**

* Show:

  * input breaking a system
* Show:

  * validation preventing issue
* Show:

  * fallback/default behavior

👉 Emphasize:

* preventing failure

---

## **5. Common Mistakes / Watch-Fors**

* assuming input is always correct
* no validation
* unclear error handling
* fragile logic

---

## **6. Tuesday Lab Bridge**

> “In lab, your goal is to identify and prevent potential issues.”

Focus:

* validation
* safe handling
* predictable behavior

---

## **7. Closing Frame**

> “A good system doesn’t just work—it handles problems gracefully.”

---

# 🧭 **WEEK 13 — WEDNESDAY CONCEPT FOCUS**

**Title:** *Week 13 — Defensive Thinking & Edge Cases*

---

## **1. Re-Anchor the Week**

> “This week is about thinking ahead—what could go wrong?”

---

## **2. Deep Concept Focus**

* Systems should expect variation
* Input can be incomplete or incorrect
* Edge cases reveal weaknesses
* Defensive thinking improves reliability

---

## **3. Common Confusion Patterns**

* “It works for me” → not tested broadly
* “I didn’t think of that case” → lack of anticipation
* “This shouldn’t happen” → real-world unpredictability

---

## **4. Thinking Tools / Heuristics**

* “What if the input is empty?”
* “What if the input is unexpected?”
* “What happens if something fails?”
* “Can this break easily?”

---

## **5. Thursday Refinement Bridge**

> “For Thursday, focus on improving how your system handles edge cases.”

Examples:

* add validation
* handle errors
* improve feedback

---

## **6. Closing Reinforcement**

> “Reliable systems are built by anticipating problems—not reacting to them.”

---

# 🧠 Instructor Notes (For You)

* underestimate risk
* think validation is optional
* miss edge cases

## Your role:

* introduce simple, practical examples
* avoid heavy security theory
* focus on usability and reliability

## Critical reinforcement:

> “You don’t need complex security—just thoughtful handling.”
