# White Paper Outline v1

## Working Title

**From Legacy Software Curriculum to AI-Era Program Design**

### Possible Subtitle Options

- A Transitional Framework for Modernizing Software Education in Wisconsin Technical Colleges
- A Bridge Model for Moving from Conventional Software Programs to AI-Era Workforce Preparation
- A Practical Transition Strategy for AI-Injected and AI-Native Software Education

---

## Purpose of the White Paper

This white paper should not try to function as a final curriculum handbook.

Its purpose is to:

- define the current statewide problem clearly
- explain why conventional software curriculum assumptions are no longer enough
- distinguish between different AI-related workforce realities
- propose a practical transition model rather than a vague call for AI addition
- position Southwest Tech's bridge approach as a credible and potentially
  transferable model
- create a pathway for future AAISE successor-program discussion without
  pretending that the final statewide design is already settled

---

## Tone and Positioning Guidance

The paper should sound:

- practical rather than speculative
- forward-looking without sounding reckless
- architecturally thoughtful without sounding overly theoretical
- influential without sounding territorial

It should frame Southwest Tech as:

- actively engaged
- grounded in current constraints
- willing to pilot and contribute
- useful as a model source, not as a unilateral authority

---

## Recommended Paper Structure

### 1. Executive Summary

**Purpose of section**

Give decision-makers a quick, high-value summary of the problem, the proposed
 direction, and why action is needed now.

**Key points to include**

- current software programs were designed in a pre-AI context
- the issue is not merely missing AI topics
- students now need preparation across multiple Workforce Models
- a practical transition model is needed
- Southwest Tech is already developing a bridge approach inside current
  constraints
- this bridge work can help inform a future AAISE-style successor program

---

### 2. The Current Problem State

**Purpose of section**

Define the problem correctly before discussing solutions.

**Key points to include**

- WTCS software-related programs are largely sans AI in their current design
- the May 11 meeting already reflects statewide recognition that the current
  state is insufficient
- the real challenge is not simply adding AI topics
- the deeper issue is that the workforce now spans multiple coexisting software
  realities

**Possible source artifacts**

- `AAISE_Current_Problem_State_v1.md`
- `AAISE_Constraints_and_Design_Realities_v2.md`

---

### 3. Workforce Models for the AI Era

**Purpose of section**

Introduce the four Workforce Models as a central framing tool early enough to
guide the rest of the paper.

**Key points to include**

- `Native`
- `AI-Assisted`
- `AI-Injected`
- `AI-Embedded`

**Why this matters**

- helps prevent the conversation from collapsing into narrow AI-tool framing
- explains why a modern program cannot optimize for only one future condition
- gives statewide stakeholders a more accurate vocabulary for curriculum design

---

### 4. What Has Changed in Software Practice

**Purpose of section**

Explain the environmental shift in a way that broad stakeholders can recognize.

**Key points to include**

- AI is changing software work at the workflow, application, and systems level
- the shift is not limited to chatbot or prompt use
- organizations are moving unevenly, creating a mixed-state workforce reality
- curriculum must prepare students for environments that range from conventional
  to AI-embedded

---

### 5. Why Traditional “Add AI” Approaches Are Not Enough

**Purpose of section**

Explain why shallow or additive curriculum change will underperform.

**Key points to include**

- AI cannot be reduced to a single later course or tool add-on
- legacy “teach everything” pedagogy often overloads curriculum without
  increasing integrative capability
- many manual tasks have shifted under higher-level abstractions, frameworks,
  and AI-supported workflows
- curriculum should be governed by capability value, not inherited coverage
  habits

**Possible source artifacts**

- `AAISE_Constraints_and_Design_Realities_v2.md`
- `AAISE_Graduate_Capability_Model_v2.md`

---

### 6. A Practical Transition Model: Bridge First, Successor Next

**Purpose of section**

Introduce the two-track logic as the paper’s structural response.

**Key points to include**

- Track 1: AI-injected bridge revision of the current program
- Track 2: future AAISE-style successor program
- the bridge supports immediate action under current governance constraints
- the successor provides a cleaner long-term AI-era architecture
- the two tracks should inform one another rather than operate independently

**Possible source artifacts**

- `AAISE_Two_Track_Transition_Model_v1.md`
- `Semester_By_Semester_Bridge_Design_v1.md`

---

### 7. What Southwest Tech Is Already Learning

**Purpose of section**

Show that this is not purely conceptual work.

**Key points to include**

- first-semester AI-injected redesign work is already underway
- alternate titles are already supporting bridge logic
- AI integration is being implemented in staged, governed ways
- instructional patterns are emerging around:
  - AI role differentiation
  - bounded AI use
  - earlier architecture and API exposure
  - AI-use justification

**Important tone note**

This section should be honest:

- the work is real
- the work is not yet fully taught and validated

That honesty strengthens rather than weakens credibility.

**Possible source artifacts**

- `Bridge_Implementation_Patterns_Already_in_Play_v1.md`
- course-level implementation artifacts such as the Python IIM

---

### 8. Proposed Graduate Capability Direction

**Purpose of section**

Show that the redesign is being guided by graduate outcomes, not just by course
 renaming.

**Key points to include**

- durable software foundations
- governed AI-assisted development
- AI role selection and justification
- AI-injected application design
- AI-embedded systems awareness
- modern data modeling and retrieval reasoning
- systems performance and abstraction awareness
- collaborative delivery and CI/CD workflow
- applied integration and demonstrated capability

**Possible source artifacts**

- `AAISE_Graduate_Capability_Model_v2.md`

---

### 9. Illustrative Developmental Progression Across the Program

**Purpose of section**

Demonstrate that the bridge model follows a coherent pedagogical arc.

**Key points to include**

- Semester 1: foundations and controlled AI entry
- Semester 2: data, stack depth, and modern development reasoning
- Semester 3: AI-injected architecture and applied system context
- Semester 4: delivery, integration, and demonstrated capability

**Use the semester identity statements**

- `I can code, think, and begin using AI under control.`
- `I understand data, tools, and modern development pathways more deeply.`
- `I can reason about AI as part of architecture, deployment, and system design.`
- `I can deliver, integrate, justify, and demonstrate applied capability.`

This section is likely one of the strongest rhetorical assets in the paper.

---

### 10. Illustrative Bridge Course Direction

**Purpose of section**

Provide a practical glimpse of how the bridge revision could look without
overloading the white paper with full curriculum detail.

**Key points to include**

- preserve and reframe strong foundational courses
- replace or redesign weaker or redundant slots
- use later semesters for stronger AI-injected integration
- preserve conventional employability while increasing AI-era relevance

**Possible source artifacts**

- `Bridge_Course_Direction_Matrix_Condensed_v1.md`
- `Bridge_Replacement_Course_Definitions_v1.md`

---

### 11. Why This Matters for Student Mobility and Institutional Transition

**Purpose of section**

Show that the transition model is not only educationally stronger, but
operationally thoughtful.

**Key points to include**

- current and future programs may overlap for at least a year
- students need manageable movement between pathways
- faculty need manageable implementation pacing
- institutions need coherent transition logic rather than abrupt replacement

This section is likely important for gaining practical support.

---

### 12. Why Southwest Tech’s Work Could Serve as a Useful Pilot Model

**Purpose of section**

Position Southwest constructively within the statewide conversation.

**Key points to include**

- Southwest is already doing early bridge work
- the work is grounded in current constraints
- the design logic is transferable even if local implementations differ
- this could help inform broader WTCS redesign discussions

**Tone note**

This section should invite collaboration, not claim ownership of the statewide
future.

---

### 13. Design Framework and Guiding Logic

**Purpose of section**

Introduce the paper’s underlying design logic without making the paper depend on
acceptance of new terminology.

**Key points to include**

- human-governed curriculum design
- constraints made explicit early
- bridge and successor logic kept distinct
- lower-level design work allowed to refine higher-order understanding
- coherence prioritized over isolated topic expansion

This is the place to use RBA concepts carefully and moderately.

The paper may or may not need to foreground the term `Refraction-Based
Architecture`, but the logic behind it is useful here.

---

### 14. Near-Term Recommendations

**Purpose of section**

End with practical, credible next steps.

**Possible recommendations**

- continue AI-injected bridge development within current approved structures
- use bridge work to inform successor-program design
- encourage pilot-oriented statewide collaboration
- identify shared capability targets across software-program redesign efforts
- avoid reducing AI modernization to tool familiarity alone

---

### 15. Conclusion

**Purpose of section**

Close with a strong, clear summary of why this matters now.

**Key points to include**

- the question is no longer whether software curriculum must change
- the question is how to redesign responsibly
- a transition architecture is needed, not just topic insertion
- Wisconsin has an opportunity to shape a more relevant software education model
- Southwest Tech may be positioned to contribute useful early structure to that
  effort

## Writing Guidance for Drafting the Full Paper

When drafting the paper, prioritize:

- readability
- clear paragraphing
- plain language over jargon
- practical examples over abstract assertion
- influence through coherence rather than through forceful claims

Avoid:

- making the paper sound like a final statewide mandate
- overloading the paper with too many course-level details
- introducing RBA so heavily that readers feel they must first learn a new
  theory before evaluating the proposal
