# Brief
## Wisconsin Salary Baseline for Junior Developers and the Case for AI-Augmented AAISE Pathways

This brief uses **Wisconsin** as the base market for estimating starting salaries for a **junior developer who has just graduated with an associate degree in software engineering or a closely related IT/software development program**. Because labor-market sources rarely isolate salary by exact associate-degree title, the estimates below use current Wisconsin data for **junior software developer, software developer, software engineer, and web developer** roles as the best available proxy.

The purpose is twofold:

- Establish a reasonable **Wisconsin starting salary baseline**
- Show why keeping the **Associate of AI Software Engineering (AAISE) / IT curriculum current with AI-augmented engineering skills** strengthens the value proposition for students and supports response to current AI program velocity concerns

## Wisconsin Baseline

A practical Wisconsin estimate for a newly graduated junior developer is:

- **Typical starting range:** `$65,000-$75,000/year`
- **Practical midpoint:** `$69,000-$72,000/year`
- **Conservative floor:** `$60,000-$65,000/year`
- **Strong first offer:** `$75,000-$85,000+`

This estimate is grounded in current statewide market data:

- Wisconsin **Software Developers** show **$63,120 at the 10th percentile**, **$82,280 at the 25th percentile**, and **$103,360 median** in 2024 BLS wage data via O*NET: [O*NET Wisconsin Software Developers wages](https://www.onetonline.org/link/localwages/15-1252.00?st=WI)
- Wisconsin **Junior Software Developer** postings on ZipRecruiter show **$67,600 at the 25th percentile** and **$89,808 average** as of **April 26, 2026**: [ZipRecruiter Junior Software Developer Wisconsin](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Salaries/Junior-Software-Developer-Salary--in-Wisconsin)
- Wisconsin **Web Developer** salary on Indeed averages **$61,729** as of **April 13, 2026**: [Indeed Web Developer Wisconsin](https://www.indeed.com/career/web-developer/salaries/WI)
- Wisconsin **Software Engineer** salary on Indeed averages **$109,696** as of **April 18, 2026**: [Indeed Software Engineer Wisconsin](https://www.indeed.com/career/software-engineer/salaries/WI)

## Breakdown 1: By City

Estimated starting pay for a junior developer in Wisconsin varies by regional labor market:

| Area | Estimated entry-level annual | Hourly equivalent |
|---|---:|---:|
| Madison | `$72,000-$82,000` | `$34.62-$39.42` |
| Milwaukee metro | `$68,000-$78,000` | `$32.69-$37.50` |
| Green Bay / Appleton / Fox Valley | `$64,000-$74,000` | `$30.77-$35.58` |
| Rural / southwest / non-metro Wisconsin | `$60,000-$70,000` | `$28.85-$33.65` |

These are consistent with metro-level wage differences shown in Wisconsin occupational wage data: [O*NET Wisconsin Software Developers wages](https://www.onetonline.org/link/localwages/15-1252.00?st=WI)

## Breakdown 2: By Role

Starting salary also depends on job title and technical expectations:

| Role | Estimated annual | Hourly equivalent |
|---|---:|---:|
| Junior Software Developer | `$67,000-$75,000` | `$32.21-$36.06` |
| Web Developer | `$60,000-$68,000` | `$28.85-$32.69` |
| Software Developer / Engineer I | `$72,000-$85,000` | `$34.62-$40.87` |
| QA / Test Automation entry-level | `$58,000-$68,000` | `$27.88-$32.69` |

This helps clarify that a student's first wage outcome may differ depending on whether they enter web development, software engineering, or testing/automation work.

## Breakdown 3: By Experience Ramp

For students and program stakeholders, the most useful view may be the early-career wage ramp:

| Experience level | Estimated annual | Hourly equivalent |
|---|---:|---:|
| 0 years, just graduated | `$65,000-$75,000` | `$31.25-$36.06` |
| 1 year experience | `$72,000-$82,000` | `$34.62-$39.42` |
| 2 years experience | `$78,000-$92,000` | `$37.50-$44.23` |

This suggests that even modest early experience can move graduates well beyond entry-level wages within the first one to two years.

## WTCS / SWTC Audience Context

For a **WTCS or SWTC audience**, market wages alone are not the only relevant lens. Program value is often discussed in relation to whether completers enter **high-wage** employment.

Two reference points matter here:

- **SWTC** classifies **high-wage programs** as those leading to jobs paying **`$25.00/hour and higher`** in its district framework: [SWTC Board Packet, March 28, 2024](https://www.swtc.edu/uploadedpdfs/about/board/Packets/2024-03-28-bp.pdf)
- **WTCS** policy and Perkins guidance define high-wage outcomes relative to the institution's or district's median graduate entry wage, rather than a single statewide number: [WTCS Policy Manual](https://mywtcs.wtcsystem.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/WTCS-Board-Policy-Manual-FINAL-April-2020.pdf), [WTCS Perkins FY 2026-27 Guidelines](https://mywtcs.wtcsystem.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/FY-2026-2027-Perkins-Grant-Guidelines-10-9-25.pdf)

Using the SWTC threshold as a plain-language benchmark:

- **`$25/hour` is about `$52,000/year`**
- The estimated junior developer starting ranges in Wisconsin are generally **above that threshold**
- Even conservative entry-level estimates usually exceed the threshold by a meaningful margin

That makes software development and related **Associate of AI Software Engineering (AAISE)** aligned pathways strong candidates for a **high-wage** classification in practical student-facing terms.

## AI-Augmented Engineers Caveat

A useful caveat is that the market is changing, and increasingly the target is not simply "developer," but **AI-augmented developer or engineer**.

The evidence supports making this point carefully:

- PwC reports that **US workers with advanced AI skills earned a 56% wage premium** in its 2025 analysis: [PwC US AI Jobs Barometer](https://www.pwc.com/us/en/tech-effect/ai-analytics/ai-jobs-barometer.html)
- PwC's earlier USA report found that job postings demanding AI skills were associated with a **25% wage premium**, and listed **applications programmers at 32%**: [PwC USA PDF](https://www.pwc.com/gx/en/issues/artificial-intelligence/job-barometer/countries/pwc-ai-jobs-barometer-usa.pdf)
- A 2026 hiring experiment found that AI skills increased interview invitation probabilities by about **8 to 15 percentage points**, including for software engineering roles: [AI Skills Improve Job Prospects](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.13286)
- Research on AI-enhanced developers argues that future-ready programs should teach both AI tool use and core engineering judgment to avoid deskilling and improve adaptability: [AI-Enhanced Software Developer Research](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.00202)

This does **not** prove that every junior graduate with AI exposure will automatically earn a higher salary. Wisconsin-specific salary tables for "AI-augmented associate-degree junior developers" do not yet exist in a clean form. However, the national evidence strongly suggests that AI capability is becoming a valuable hiring and wage signal.

For the **Associate of AI Software Engineering (AAISE) degree**, that strengthens the argument that curriculum modernization is not optional. It is part of maintaining program relevance, employability, and wage competitiveness.

## Bottom Line

The Wisconsin market supports a reasonable starting estimate of **`$65,000-$75,000/year`** for a newly graduated junior developer from an associate-level pathway, with many roles already landing comfortably above the **`$25/hour` high-wage benchmark** used by SWTC.

The three Wisconsin breakdowns show the same basic pattern:

- By **city**, wages are strongest in Madison and Milwaukee but remain competitive across the state
- By **role**, software engineering and software developer tracks generally outperform web-only and lower-end QA entry points
- By **experience**, graduates can move from solid entry wages to substantially stronger compensation within one to two years

The added AI caveat makes the case stronger, not weaker:

- The near-term goal is no longer just to produce entry-level developers
- The stronger workforce goal is to produce **AI-augmented junior engineers** who can adapt to changing tools, workflows, and employer expectations
- That framing supports continued investment in current IT/AAISE programming and helps justify action tied to the **AI Program Velocity Briefing**
